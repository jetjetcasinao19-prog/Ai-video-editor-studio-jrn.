# Brand voice — How it Started

**Every Legend Has a Beginning.** The channel tells the origin of things, in
general: how people, businesses, inventions, places, traditions and moments in
history began — where it started, the struggle, the turning point.

Used by the publisher agent and the "edit this" workflow when writing hook
titles, descriptions, hashtags and comment replies. Edit freely.

## Audience
- **Target market: the United States.** Curious US viewers (about 16–34:
  students, young workers, dreamers) who love "how did this start?" stories,
  comebacks and surprising facts.
- Topics (the origin of things, in general):
  - **People:** famous people, athletes, creators — before they were famous
  - **Businesses & brands:** how companies, products and logos started
  - **History:** how events, countries, cities, traditions and holidays began
  - **Things & ideas:** inventions, foods, words, sports, games, habits and
    everyday objects — "why is it like this?"
  - **and more:** any "how it started" story with a real, checkable beginning
- Language: **US English only** (American spelling: "color", "favorite";
  US references: dollars, miles, US holidays and brands when it fits).
  No Tagalog in titles, captions, descriptions or hashtags.
- Posting times: plan in **US Eastern Time (ET)** and also show the owner's
  Philippine time (PHT = ET + 13 hours; + 12 hours after US daylight saving
  starts in March). The owner posts by hand from the Philippines.

## Tone
- Storyteller: dramatic but true. Build up from the humble start to the turning
  point. Inspiring, never preachy. Talk like a friend telling a great story.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use "you". Avoid jargon; explain any technical word in a few words.

## Hook titles (hook card / pop-up, max ~40 characters)
- The hook is the **first spoken line** and the **3–7 big words on screen in
  the first second**. 85% of viewers swiped away in Nov 2026 when videos opened
  on dates, names or slow scenery. Name the thing and the surprise at once.
- Lead with the surprising beginning: "He was rejected 30 times", "It started
  in a garage", "This snack was a mistake", "Why Monday starts the week".
- Short, curiosity first, no spoilers of the ending. No clickbait the video
  doesn't deliver.

## Descriptions
- 1–3 short lines: what they will learn, then a call to action.
- Calls to action to rotate: "Subscribe for more origin stories",
  "Whose beginning should we tell next? Comment below", "Every legend has a beginning."
- Always state facts that can be checked; the researcher agent keeps sources.

## Hashtags
- 3–8 per post: 2–3 broad, 2–3 specific to the topic, plus the brand hashtags
  from `brand.json`.

## Never
- Never invent facts, quotes or numbers about real people or brands.
- Never promise results you can't show. Never copy other creators' text.
- Never use music, clips or logos you don't have the right to use.
