# Brand voice — MindTrace

**Why You Think What You Think.** The channel explains the human mind: the
psychology experiments, biases, habits and brain quirks behind everyday
behavior — where the idea came from, what the study really found, and one
thing you can do with it.

Used by the publisher agent, the "edit this" workflow and the animated-short
skill (INKBLOT style) when writing hook titles, descriptions, hashtags and
comment replies. Edit freely.

## Audience
- **Target market: the United States.** Curious US viewers (about 16–34:
  students, young workers, self-improvers) who love "why does my brain do
  that?" moments, mind tricks and surprising studies.
- Topics: famous experiments, cognitive biases, persuasion, habits and
  procrastination, memory, emotions, relationships, sleep and dreams, dark
  psychology (explained, never taught as manipulation).
- Language: **US English only** (American spelling: "color", "behavior").
  No Tagalog in titles, captions, descriptions or hashtags.
- Posting times: plan in **US Eastern Time (ET)** and also show the owner's
  Philippine time (PHT = ET + 13 hours; + 12 hours after US daylight saving
  starts in March). The owner posts by hand from the Philippines.

## Tone
- A sharp friend who reads the studies so you don't have to: curious,
  a little mysterious, never preachy. Case-file feel ("the study", "the catch").
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use "you". Explain any technical word in a few words.

## Hook titles (hook card / pop-up, max ~40 characters)
- The hook is the **first spoken line** and the **3–7 big words on screen in
  the first second**. 85% of viewers swiped away in Nov 2026 when videos opened
  on dates, names or slow scenery. Name the thing and the surprise at once.
- Lead with the surprising effect on *you*: "Your brain keeps a hidden list",
  "One word makes people say yes", "Why you remember the bad day".
- Curiosity first, no spoilers. No clickbait the video doesn't deliver.

## Descriptions
- 1–3 short lines: the effect, the study behind it, one thing to try; then a
  call to action.
- Calls to action to rotate: "Subscribe to trace your mind",
  "Which mind trick should we trace next? Comment below",
  "Follow for more mind tricks."
- Name the study (researcher + year) so it can be checked; the researcher
  agent keeps sources. Mention when later research found mixed results.

## Hashtags
- 3–8 per post: 2–3 broad (#Psychology, #PsychologyFacts, #MindTricks),
  2–3 specific to the topic, plus the brand hashtags from `brand.json`.

## Never
- Never invent studies, numbers or quotes. Never overstate a finding
  ("proves", "always") — say what the study found.
- No medical or mental-health diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Never teach manipulation; explain it so viewers can spot it.
- Never use music, clips or logos you don't have the right to use.
