Your brain is keeping a to-do list... you never wrote.

Berlin, the 1920s. The story goes, psychologist Kurt Lewin is in a café with his students. A waiter takes a huge order. No notepad. He remembers every single item.

Then the bill is paid. They ask him again. He can't remember a thing.

Lewin's student, Bluma Zeigarnik, can't let it go. So she tests it. People do small tasks: puzzles, beads, clay. Some get stopped halfway.

Later, they remember the interrupted tasks almost twice as well.

An unfinished task stays open in your mind... like a tab you can't close. That's why cliffhangers hook you. And why that unanswered text bugs you at night.

It's called the Zeigarnik effect. So here's the trick. Can't start something? Just start it. Your brain will want to finish.
