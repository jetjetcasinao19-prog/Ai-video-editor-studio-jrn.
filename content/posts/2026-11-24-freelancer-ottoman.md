- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Freelancer sounds modern. It actually means a soldier for hire, with a lance."
- **Summary:** "Freelancer" first meant a hired soldier or knight with a lance who served whoever paid. The earliest
  written evidence dictionaries cite is Sir Walter Scott's novel Ivanhoe (published December 1819, title page dated
  1820), where the knight Maurice de Bracy speaks of his "Free Lances". The word later came to describe politicians tied
  to no party, and then anyone who works for themselves. Captions give no dates for the later senses.

  (2) Walter Scott Digital Archive (University of Edinburgh): Ivanhoe was published in Edinburgh on 18 December 1819

  used, matching the on-screen card.
