# Song Stuck in Your Head? Grab Gum — The Earworm Gum Trick, INKBLOT short

- **Channel:** MindTrace (@MINDTRACEOFFICIAL-YT) — brand kit `brand/mindtrace/`
- **Video:** `animate/projects/earworm-gum/out/earworm-gum-inkblot.mp4`
  (34 s, 9:16, 1080x1920, voice-over by the owner (ElevenLabs voice "Bradford"),
  end card: MindTrace case-file cover (Case No. 2015), stamped "CASE: OPEN" and
  "Subscribe to trace your mind"). 34 s is fine for all four platforms.
- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Song stuck in your head? Grab a piece of gum."
- **Summary:** In 2015, psychologists at the University of Reading played people catchy
  pop songs, then asked them not to think about them. People chewing gum heard the song
  in their heads less often. The trick: replaying a song uses your inner voice, like
  silently singing. Chewing jams that system — less room for the chorus. So next time
  it's on repeat: chew.
- **Edit notes:** a 2D animated short drawn entirely in code in the INKBLOT style
  (Rorschach-style living ink blots, psychology case-file look, red-pen notes).
  On-screen: red music notes looping inside a head ("on repeat…") and a pack of gum;
  "2015", "University of Reading (that's in England)", three people in headphones with
  notes; a "DON'T THINK ABOUT THE SONG" sign while notes leak out anyway; two heads, no
  gum vs chewing gum, with tally marks ("less often", marked as an illustration); a tiny
  singing mouth inside the head ("inner voice", "silently singing"); gum filling that
  inner mouth, stamped "JAMMED", the chorus falling away; a spinning repeat symbol
  stopped by a gum pack: "CHEW." No real songs are named or played. Voice is the owner's
  own voice-over.
- **Audience:** United States (US English; times in content/schedule.md are in ET with Philippine time).
- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Beaman, C. P., Powell, K., & Rapley, E. (2015). "Want to block earworms from
    conscious awareness? B(u)y gum!" *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*,
    68(6), 1049–1057. University of Reading, UK.
  - Participants heard catchy songs and were then asked not to think about them (and,
    in another study, were free to); those chewing gum reported fewer "earworm" thoughts
    of the song (and fewer heard/sung instances) than those not chewing.
  - The explanation is that chewing occupies the articulatory/subvocal system used to
    "replay" music in your head (the paper's account). The effect is modest; say "can
    help," not "always works."
  - The tally marks are an illustration, not the study's counts. No songs are named.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
