- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Superglue was a failure at first."
- **Summary:** Harry Coover, a chemist at Eastman Kodak, was working on clear plastic for gun sights during World War II. A cyanoacrylate material stuck to everything it touched, so it was rejected as too sticky. In 1951, at Kodak's plant in Kingsport, Tennessee, Coover's group (with colleague Fred Joyner) came back to it, and the glue stuck lab equipment together (the narration says it glued expensive lab equipment; the on-screen detail is refractometer prisms). Coover realized it was a useful adhesive. It went on sale in 1958 as Eastman #910 (later Eastman 910).

- **Fact check (from WebSearch result summaries; I could not open any page in full, so open each link once before publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) Coover worked with cyanoacrylate while searching for a clear plastic for gun sights, and the material was rejected as too sticky (Wikipedia on Coover and Cyanoacrylate, Lemelson-MIT, Hobart and William Smith Colleges).
  (2) 1951: Coover was at Kodak's chemical plant in Kingsport, Tennessee (Tennessee Eastman, a Kodak subsidiary), working on heat-resistant polymers for jet canopies, and he re-discovered the cyanoacrylates with Fred Joyner (Lemelson-MIT; Wikipedia names Joyner).
  (3) Moisture sets it: rapid anionic polymerization triggered by surface moisture explains the instant bond (2018 Molecules review, PMC6017548; University of Bristol "Molecule of the Month").
  (4) 1958: first sold as "Eastman #910", later "Eastman 910" (Wikipedia on Superglue and Coover).
  Items to flag:
  (1) DATE "1942" IS CONTESTED. Wikipedia and other sources say 1942 and Eastman Kodak. The Hobart and William Smith Colleges page says 1944 for the gun-sight search, and Lemelson-MIT says Coover joined Eastman Kodak's chemical division in Rochester shortly after his 1944 PhD. If 1944 is right, the "WARTIME · 1942" seal and the narration's "1942" are off by two years. Copy below says "during World War II" and avoids the year 1942. Recommend re-rendering the seal as "WARTIME · 1940s" and changing the narration to "During World War II" if easy; otherwise cite it as "reported as 1942".
  (2) "KODAK" in 1942 vs 1951. Eastman Kodak (Rochester, NY) for the wartime work is consistent with the sources. In 1951 the work was at Kodak's Kingsport, Tennessee plant (Tennessee Eastman Company, a Kodak subsidiary). The narration says "his team" and the card says "EASTMAN KODAK LABS" at the start, which is fine; the 1951 scene has no company name. Copy says "Kodak's plant in Kingsport, Tennessee".
  (3) RESOLVED: the callout now reads "COSTLY LAB GLASS, GLUED SHUT", which fits every account. REFRACTOMETER: One search result summary says Joyner (and Shearer, per another account) found the glass prism faces of a refractometer bonded while measuring the refractive index of freshly made ethyl cyanoacrylate; another says Joyner spread the compound between lenses on a "reflectometer". Sources differ on the instrument and on who else was involved. A second search found nothing more specific. Copy says "lab equipment" and, in the pinned comment, "reportedly the prisms of an instrument that measures how light bends". Do not state who exactly did it. The narration's "his team" is safe.
