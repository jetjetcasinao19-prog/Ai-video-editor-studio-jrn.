**Description:**
The board game about getting rich started as a protest. Lizzie Magie (say "McGee") patented The Landlord's Game in 1904. Players buy property, charge rent, and watch others go broke, and she wanted people to see how unfair that is. For years, people made their own homemade copies, and players in Atlantic City added their own street names. Then, in the 1930s, Charles Darrow sold a version to Parker Brothers. He was credited as the inventor, and royalties made him a millionaire. Parker Brothers bought Lizzie's patent for $500. The truth only came out in the 1970s, during a court fight over another board game. A game about unfairness with an unfair origin story.
