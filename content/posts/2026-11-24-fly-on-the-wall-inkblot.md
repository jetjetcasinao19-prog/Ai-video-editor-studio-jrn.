- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Mischkowski, D., Kross, E., & Bushman, B. J. (2012). "Flies on the wall are
    less aggressive: Self-distancing 'in the heat of the moment' reduces aggressive
    thoughts, angry feelings and aggressive behavior." *Journal of Experimental Social
    Psychology*, 48(5), 1187–1191.
  - Participants were provoked with insulting feedback from the experimenter while doing
    a hard task, and were told to take either a self-immersed (own eyes) or a
    self-distanced (fly on the wall) perspective. The distanced group reported less anger
    and gave weaker noise blasts to their provoker in a later reaction-time game
    (the aggression measure). The paper's point is that distancing works "in the heat of
    the moment," not only when looking back later.
  - Talking to yourself by name: Kross, E., et al. (2014). "Self-talk as a regulatory
    mechanism: How you do it matters." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*,
    106(2), 304–324. Using your own name or "you" instead of "I" helped people stay
    calmer under stress. "Alex" is just an example name.
  - The bars on screen are an illustration, not the study's numbers. These are helpful
    habits, not a treatment for anger problems; if anger is causing harm, talk to a
    professional.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
