# Velcro Came From a Dog Walk — Ottoman miniature short, tile look, "book of marvels"

- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Velcro was invented because of a dog walk."
- **Summary:** Swiss engineer George (also spelled Georges) de Mestral came home from a walk in the mountains with his
  dog covered in burrs. Velcro Companies' own history dates this to 1941. Under a microscope he saw tiny hooks catching
  loops in fabric and fur, and spent years copying it as one strip of hooks and one strip of loops. He named it from the
  French words for velvet (velours) and hook (crochet). It was patented in the 1950s. Captions give no exact patent year.

- **Fact check (from WebSearch result summaries; I could not open any page in full, so open each link once before
  publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) 1941: Velcro Companies' own story page says that in 1941 de Mestral returned from an Alpine hunting trip with burrs
  stuck to his wool pants and his dog's fur, and spent nearly a decade trying to copy the mechanism. WIPO also places it
  on one summer morning in 1941. Hackaday and McGill give 1941 too.
  (2) Burrs, microscope, hooks and loops: Lemelson-MIT, WIPO, Hackaday and McGill describe him examining a burr under a
  microscope and seeing many small hooks that catch on loops of thread or animal fur.
  (3) The name: Velcro is a blend of French "velours" (velvet) and "crochet" (hook), per Wikipedia, Velcro's page summary,
  Lemelson-MIT, WIPO and APS. The on-screen VEL + CRO split matches.
  (4) Patent: Swiss application filed 1951 (CH295638, WIPO). Grant year differs: WIPO says 1954 in Switzerland, Wikipedia
  says 1955; the US and French patents followed in 1955 (WIPO). The card "PATENTED IN THE 1950s" is true under every
  version, so captions say only "the 1950s". The trademark was registered May 13, 1958 (APS, via search summary); not used.
  Items to FIX or keep out of captions:
  (a) Year and place of the walk: the voice-over says "In the 1940s ... mountains", the card says 1941 and shows the Alps.
  Velcro's own page says Alpine and 1941. APS News instead dates a burr episode to 1948 in the Jura Mountains with an
  Irish pointer, and Live Science gives 1948. Sources disagree, so captions say "in the 1940s" and "in the mountains",
  and the 1941 date appears only as "Velcro's own history dates it to 1941" in the YouTube description. Consider
  changing the "1941" card only if you want zero conflict.
  (b) "A dog walk": Velcro's own page and others call it a hunting trip or hike. The title and voice-over say "dog walk".
  Captions say "a walk in the mountains with his dog" and never "hunting". The title matches what the video shows.
  (c) Spelling: sources split. Lemelson-MIT, Encyclopedia.com, Wikipedia and the on-screen card use "George"; swissinfo and
  History.com use "Georges", as does the voice-over. The YouTube description says "Georges de Mestral (also spelled
  George)"; other captions use "George de Mestral" to match the card.
  (d) "Hooks and loops copied from nature" is the standard account. How the first hooks were made differs by source
  (cutting loops, nylon under infrared light), and "nearly a decade" of development is Velcro's own wording. None of
  that is in the captions.
  (e) The dog, burdock plant, brass microscope, tiles and the "RRRIP!" opening are artistic.
  (f) Series: "Accidental Inventions". It fits better than "Brand Origins" because the story is a chance observation on a
  walk (like Bubble Wrap, Post-it Notes and Play-Doh), and the video is about the invention, not the company. Added to
  content/playlists.md.

  versions appeared, I did not see the en URL), APS News (https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2004/02/death-george-mestral-velcro),

Swiss engineer Georges de Mestral (also spelled George) came home from a walk in the mountains with his dog, both covered in burrs. Under a microscope, he saw tiny hooks catching tiny loops in fabric and fur. Velcro Companies' own history dates the walk to 1941, though some sources give a later year. The invention was patented in the 1950s.
