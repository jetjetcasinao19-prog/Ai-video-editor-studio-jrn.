- **Summary:** For centuries the Trojan War was thought to be a myth from Homer. Frank Calvert, a diplomat

- **Fact check (WebSearch summaries; I could not open any page in full, so re-check the wording before
  publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) Britannica's Schliemann summary: in 1873 at Hisarlik he found the remains of ancient Troy and a gold
  treasure he called "Priam's Treasure", which he smuggled out of the country. It also says his written
  accounts contain many self-serving fabrications.
  (2) UNESCO listing 849 (inscribed 1998): says Schliemann undertook the first excavations in 1870 and
  describes the "Schliemann Trench", with Troy II houses at the bottom. So "1870" holds; other sources date
  the main campaigns 1871-73 (the trench is dated 1871-1890 on Wikipedia, since he kept deepening it).
  (3) Treasure layer: found in what Schliemann identified as Troy II, dated by later work to about 2400 BC
  (the on-screen card "c. 2400 BC" is in range; I saw c. 2500-2300 BC in the usual reckoning). Priam would
  belong to Troy VI or VIIa (Late Bronze Age, roughly 1300-1180 BC). Gap is over 1,000 years, so
  "~1,000+ YEARS" holds.
  (4) Pushkin Museum, Moscow: acknowledged in 1994 that it held the gold (identified 1993); most of the
  hoard is there today. Some pieces are reportedly in Istanbul. Kept out of captions (not in the video).
  (5) The Ottoman government sued Schliemann in Athens in 1874; the case was settled in 1875. The final
  sum is reported inconsistently (10,000 gold francs vs 50,000 gold francs), so no amount appears anywhere.
  Items to FIX or keep out of captions:
  (a) CALVERT'S TITLE: "a British official" in the voice-over is loose. Sources disagree: the British Museum
  calls him American Consular Agent at the Dardanelles who sometimes also acted as British Consul; EBSCO says
  U.S. vice-consul; Wikipedia says acting British consul in 1856 and 1858. He was an Englishman born on
  Malta. All captions say "a diplomat living in the region" instead. Consider re-recording as "a British-born
  diplomat, Frank Calvert".
  (b) CALVERT "ON ADVICE": sources say Calvert did exploratory digs on Hisarlik before Schliemann and that
  Schliemann was pointed to him in 1868. A 2005 review notes Schliemann later reworded his account to make
  the Hisarlik idea look like his own. Captions say Calvert "was convinced", never that he owned the land
  (seen only on Wikiwand).
  (c) TRENCH SIZE "ABOUT 40 M WIDE · 17 M DEEP": the 17 m depth is consistent across sources (Wikipedia,
  World History Encyclopedia). The 40 m width appears in World History Encyclopedia's image page and
  Madain Project, while another source speaks of a 70 m wide area dug in 1872, so the width is not firmly
  settled. The card says "ABOUT", which is fair, but the numbers are kept out of every caption.
  (d) DATE OF THE DISCOVERY: sources give 31 May, 15 June or 17 June 1873; only the year is used.
  (e) "TOSSING AWAY LAYERS HE THINKS ARE TOO YOUNG": supported in general (he believed Homer's Troy lay in
  the lowest levels and dug down to bedrock, damaging upper layers), but I did not find a single quotable
  source for his reasoning. It stays in the video; captions say only that the trench "cut through" later
  layers.
  (f) "MAY HAVE DESTROYED THE REAL TROY": an opinion in the hook. Britannica and others say his work was
  damaging but also genuine. Captions keep "may have" and "damaged".
  (g) Series: put in "Landmarks" (see content/playlists.md). Reason: the story is about a place and what is
  still standing there (the trench and UNESCO site), like the Terracotta Army (also a discovery story).
  "Ancient Origins" is for ancient events and legends told as events (Marathon, Thermopylae, Hannibal);
  Troy's war itself is not shown, only its excavation in the 1800s.

  and UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Archaeological Site of Troy" (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/849).
