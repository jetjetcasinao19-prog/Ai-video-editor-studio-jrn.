- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Radvansky, G. A., Krawietz, S. A., & Tamplin, A. K. (2011). "Walking through
    doorways causes forgetting: Further explorations." *Quarterly Journal of Experimental
    Psychology*, 64(8), 1632–1645. University of Notre Dame.
  - Participants carried objects between tables (in a virtual environment and in real
    rooms) and were tested on what they were carrying. Memory was worse after moving
    through a doorway than after walking the same distance within one room.
  - The explanation (the Event Horizon Model) is that a doorway is an "event boundary":
    the brain closes off the old event and starts a new one, so the old one is harder to
    reach. "Files the old one away" is a plain-English version of that. The effect is
    small and doesn't always replicate; say "can make you forget," not "always."
  - "Say it out loud" is our tip, not part of this study. It matches the separate
    "production effect": saying a word aloud makes it easier to remember than reading it
    silently (MacLeod et al., 2010).
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
