- **Fact check:**
  - Sources: Ewen Montagu, *The Man Who Never Was* (1953); Ben Macintyre, *Operation
    Mincemeat* (2010); UK National Archives files on the operation (released 1996).
  - In April 1943, British intelligence (Ewen Montagu and Charles Cholmondeley) dressed
    the body of Glyndwr Michael, a Welsh homeless man who had died after swallowing rat
    poison, as "Captain (Acting Major) William Martin, Royal Marines."
  - The fake letters suggested the Allies would invade Greece and Sardinia, with Sicily
    only a cover target. The "pocket litter" included love letters and a photo from a
    fiancée called "Pam", theatre ticket stubs, and a bank overdraft letter.
  - HMS *Seraph*, a submarine, released the body off Huelva, Spain, on 30 April 1943. The
    Spanish let German agents copy the letters before returning them.
  - Hitler reinforced Greece, Sardinia and the Balkans (including moving a panzer
    division to Greece and sending Rommel to Greece). The Allies invaded Sicily on 10
    July 1943 with lighter losses than feared.
