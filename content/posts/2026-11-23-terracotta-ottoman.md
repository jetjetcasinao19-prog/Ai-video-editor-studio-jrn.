- **Fact check:** captions stay to what the voice-over says and what sources confirm.
  Confirmed through WebSearch summaries: farmers digging a well found the site in March 1974 near
  Xi'an (Britannica; UNESCO says the figures were found in 1974 and thousands more probably remain
  buried); the site is at the foot of Mount Li in Shaanxi, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List
  in 1987 as listing 441 (criterion vi ties it to the 221 BC unification); Qin Shi Huang, first
  emperor (reigned 221-210 BC), died in 210 BC (Britannica); the army was made to protect him in the
  afterlife (National Geographic, Wikipedia); Britannica says the army faces east; National Geographic
  says faces were shaped in molds, then the sculptor added details (hair, ears, eyebrows, mustaches,
  beards), and History.com agrees details were added by hand after molding; Guinness World Records says
  figures vary in height by role with generals tallest. Wikipedia names Yang Zhifa, his brothers and a
  neighbor, Wang Puzhi, as the diggers (sources vary on how many brothers).
