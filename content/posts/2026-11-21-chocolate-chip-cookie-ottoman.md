- **Summary:** You've heard the chocolate chip cookie was an accident. It wasn't.
  In the 1930s in Whitman, Massachusetts, Ruth Wakefield ran a popular restaurant
  called the Toll House Inn. She was a trained dietitian, and she knew exactly what
  she was doing. She chopped up a bar of Nestlé chocolate and mixed the pieces into
  her cookie dough. The cookies were a hit. In 1938, she put the recipe in her
  cookbook. Then Nestlé made her a deal: the recipe and the Toll House name printed
  on their packaging. Her payment? One dollar, which she said she never got, and
  free chocolate for life. Soon, Nestlé started selling chocolate chips, a chef's
  plan that became an American classic.
