  Thanksgiving. Her name was Sarah Josepha Hale. In the 1800s, Thanksgiving was a
  mess. Every state picked its own day, and many didn't celebrate it at all.
  Sarah ran one of America's most popular magazines, Godey's Lady's Book, and used
  it to push for one national day of thanks. She wrote to presidents: Taylor,
  Fillmore, Pierce and Buchanan. None of them acted. She didn't quit. With the
  country torn apart by the Civil War, she wrote one more letter, to Abraham
  Lincoln. Days later, Lincoln declared a national Thanksgiving, the last Thursday
  in November. Message: one woman, one pen, and a holiday the whole country shares.
