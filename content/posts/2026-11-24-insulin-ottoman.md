- **ACTION NEEDED BEFORE UPLOAD: the last on-screen card is not safely sourced.** The line "Insulin does not belong to me, it
  belongs to the world" is widely attributed to Banting, but a fact-check found no evidence he said it. Its likeliest origin
  is a 1959 children's biography (I.E. Levine), written 18 years after his death, and it does not appear in Michael Bliss's
  biography or other adult biographies. Some sites (including the Nobel Prize site and U of T's insulin100 site) repeat it
  with no primary source, and the wording varies between sites. Because the rules say never invent quotes, I recommend
  re-rendering without that card (end on the $1 coin / "TO THE UNIVERSITY OF TORONTO · 1923" card, or use a factual card such
  as "THREE INVENTORS · ONE DOLLAR EACH"). If the owner keeps it, the card should read as attributed ("Widely attributed to
  Banting"), and no caption below repeats it as a fact. This also matters for the "titles and thumbnail match the video"
  rule only in the sense of not presenting an unverified quote as real.
- **Fact check (from WebSearch result summaries; I could not open any page in full, so open each link once before
  publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) 1921, Toronto; Banting and Best; Collip purified the extract (Collip's refinement is credited in the Nobel committee's
  1923 presentation speech and in U of T / Nobel Prize pages); Macleod was the lab head.
  (2) Leonard Thompson, age 14, Toronto General Hospital: first injection 11 January 1922 (Banting and Best extract, impure,
  caused an allergic reaction; one source says the dose was too small instead). Second series from 23 January 1922 used
  Collip's purified extract and worked (blood sugar fell, ketones disappeared). He lived about 13 more years on insulin and
  died in 1935. The video says only "first injections and starts to recover", which is fair; captions below mention that the
  second, purer extract is what worked. Do not say the very first dose worked.
  (3) Patent sale: U.S. patent no. 1,469,994 (issued Oct. 9, 1923) names Banting, Best and Collip as inventors, assigned to the
  Governors of the University of Toronto for $1 each. Per the search summary, Banting IS named on it (my brief suspected he
  might not be). Macleod was NOT one of the three who sold, so "the inventors sell their patents ... one dollar each" is right
  for three people; captions say "the three inventors" and do not claim Macleod sold his. Dates vary by source: the
  agreement is signed 19 Dec 1922 in one U of T archive page, and recorded 1 Jan 1923 in others (hence "1923" in the video).
  Captions say "1923" only. Open the U of T assignment document to confirm the signatories.
  (4) Before insulin, a near-starvation diet (Allen and Joslin regimens, c. 1915 to 1922) was the leading treatment: it could
  extend some lives, but was not a cure (Mazur 2011, Nutrition Journal, via PMC). The U of T / Nobel framing "diabetes death
  sentence" is also used by the Nobel Prize site. The video's "basically a death sentence" is therefore supported in spirit;
  captions use softer wording.
  (5) 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine: awarded to Banting and Macleod "for the discovery of insulin" (Nobel
  Foundation). Best and Collip did NOT receive the prize; Banting shared his prize money with Best. (I believe Macleod shared
  with Collip, but no result I saw says so, so it is not claimed.) The omission of Best and Collip is long debated. The video
  does not mention the Nobel, and neither do the captions; add it to the pinned comment only.
  Items to flag or keep out of captions:
  (a) The Banting quote (see above): kept out of every caption.
  (b) "Death sentence" is soft in captions; "no claims about today's insulin prices" - none are made. Nothing here is medical
  advice.
  (c) Exact patent-sale date (19 Dec 1922 vs 1 Jan 1923 vs 25 Jan 1922 in one PMC account): captions give "1923" only.
  (d) The silver dollar, ledger page, flasks, hospital bed and snowy building are artistic, not documentary.
  (e) Series: new "Medicine Firsts" in content/playlists.md (see that file). Stethoscope, Pacemaker and Penicillin were not
  moved (see report); their Series lines are unchanged.
  (f) Sources, URLs taken directly from WebSearch results: University of Toronto Libraries, "The Manufacture of Insulin"
  (https://fisherdigitus.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/insulin100/manufacture-of-insulin); Nobel Prize in Physiology or
  Medicine 1923 (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1923/summary/). Also seen, not cited: U of T "Leonard Thompson"
  (https://fisherdigitus.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/insulin100/early-patients/leonard-thompson), U of T "First Clinical
