# They Don't Force You. They Choose Your Default. — The Default Effect, INKBLOT short

- **Channel:** MindTrace (@MINDTRACEOFFICIAL-YT) — brand kit `brand/mindtrace/`
- **Video:** `animate/projects/default-effect/out/default-effect-inkblot.mp4`
  (40 s, 9:16, 1080x1920, voice-over by the owner (ElevenLabs voice "Bradford"),
  end card: MindTrace case-file cover (Case No. 2003), stamped "CASE: OPEN" and
  "Subscribe to trace your mind"). 40 s is fine for all four platforms.
- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Governments don't need to force you. They just choose your default."
- **Summary:** Germany and Austria are next-door neighbors with the same language and a
  similar culture. In the early 2000s, Germans had to check a box to become organ donors:
  only 12% were. In Austria you were a donor unless you checked a box to opt out: almost
  100% were. Most people never change the default. Whoever picks it, picks for you. So
  read every pre-checked box.
- **Edit notes:** a 2D animated short drawn entirely in code in the INKBLOT style
  (Rorschach-style living ink blots, psychology case-file look, red-pen notes).
  On-screen: "force" crossed out in red pen and an "OFFICIAL FORM" with a box that ticks
  itself ("already filled in for you", "DEFAULT"); two houses side by side with waving
  German and Austrian flags and matching "Hallo!" bubbles ("same language", "similar
  culture"); "EARLY 2000s" and Germany's form "ORGAN DONOR?" with an empty box ("empty by
  default") → 12 of 100 dots red; Austria's form with "Yes, I am a donor" already ticked
  ("unless you act") → 99.98% of the dots red, with "Germany: 12%" beside it; a wide road
  marked DEFAULT that everyone walks while a thin "change" path stays empty; a figure on
  puppet strings whose box ticks itself ("picks for you"); a sign-up form with
  pre-checked boxes (offers, share my data, auto-renew, terms), a magnifying glass, and
  "share my data" unticked in red pen ("READ IT"). Voice is the owner's own voice-over.
- **Audience:** United States (US English; times in content/schedule.md are in ET with Philippine time).
- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Johnson, E. J., & Goldstein, D. (2003). "Do Defaults Save Lives?" *Science*,
    302(5649), 1338–1339. Effective organ-donor consent rates: Germany (opt-in) 12%,
    Austria (opt-out) 99.98%. "Almost 100%" is accurate.
  - These are consent rates (registered or presumed willingness), not the number of
    organs actually donated. Actual donation rates differ by much less, because families,
    hospitals and infrastructure also matter. Say "agreed to be donors," not "donated."
  - "Most people never change the default" is the paper's explanation (sticking with
    defaults takes no effort and can feel like a recommendation). It is a strong, widely
    replicated effect, but not universal.
  - Germany has changed its system since (online registry, 2022 law; still opt-in). Our
    numbers are the early-2000s figures from the paper, as the voice-over says.
  - "Governments don't need to force you" is framing: defaults are a "nudge" (Thaler &
    Sunstein). Opt-out donation isn't sinister in itself; the video's point is to notice
    who picks your defaults.
  - The sign-up form on screen is made up; no real company is shown. Flags are drawn
    simply to label the countries.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
- **Pinned comment / question:** "Which pre-checked box got you? Newsletter, auto-renew, or 'share my data'? 👇"
