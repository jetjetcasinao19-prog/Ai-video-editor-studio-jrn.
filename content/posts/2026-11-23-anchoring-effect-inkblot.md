**Description:**
In 1974, Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky spun a wheel that stopped on 10 or 65, then asked people what percent of UN countries are in Africa. The median guess was about 25% after seeing 10 and about 45% after seeing 65. A random number, almost double the answer.
That's the anchoring effect: the first number you see becomes a starting point, and you don't move far enough from it. Source: Tversky & Kahneman (1974), "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases", Science. The $5,000 offer and the price tag in the video are illustrations.
