- **Fact check (from WebSearch result summaries; I could not open any page in full, so open each link once before
  publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) October 16, 1846, the surgical amphitheater in the Bulfinch Building at Massachusetts General Hospital: first public
  demonstration of inhaled ether as a surgical anesthetic (later called "Ether Day"). Source: MGH Paul S. Russell Museum page,
  Smithsonian Magazine, Linda Hall Library.
  (2) William T. G. Morton, a Boston dentist, administered the ether. Confirmed in the same sources.
  (3) Patient: Edward Gilbert Abbott (1825-1855); the video says "Gilbert Abbott", which matches the common short form. His
  trade is given as printer (Wikipedia) or housepainter (Linda Hall Library, Harvard Gazette); the video and captions give no
  trade.
  (4) John Collins Warren removed a tumor. The location varies by source: neck (most accounts, and the video), left side of the
  jaw (Wikipedia), a vascular "erectile" tumor on the face or neck per the MGH-related paper, and one paper says tongue.
  Bigelow's 1846 account (Boston Medical and Surgical Journal, Nov 18, 1846) describes an incision near the lower jaw.
  Captions therefore say "a tumor" and "neck" only as the video does, and the pinned comment notes that sources differ.  (5) Ether Dome: the operating theater was nicknamed the "Ether Dome". Used for surgery from 1821 to about 1867/68 (sources
  differ by a year), with 8,000+ operations there per MGH museum. It is still standing, accessible to the public as a lecture
  hall, and is a National Historic Landmark. "Still standing at Mass General today" is correct.
  (6) "The start of modern anesthesia": fair as the first PUBLIC demonstration. Context, kept to the pinned comment and not
  claimed as erased: Crawford Long, a Georgia physician, reportedly used ether as early as 1842 but did not publish until
  December 1849; Horace Wells's nitrous oxide demonstration in Boston (about January 1845) failed (venue disputed: a 2013
  Anesthesiology article says probably a public hall, not the Ether Dome).
  (7) "Awake for every second" is the voice's framing; alcohol and opium were sometimes given, so captions say "usually" or
  "little relief" and never "no relief at all".
