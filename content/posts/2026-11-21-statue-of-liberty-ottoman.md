Lady Liberty once looked like a shiny new penny. When she was unveiled in New York Harbor in October 1886, she was covered in about 300 thin copper sheets, roughly as thick as two pennies. But copper changes in the sea air, and year by year she turned from brown to green. In 1906, Congress set aside money to paint her and make her look new again. People were furious, and one newspaper called it sacrilege. The plan was dropped. That green layer, called a patina, actually protects the copper underneath: a flaw that became her famous color.
