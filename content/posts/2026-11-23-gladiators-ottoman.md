**Description:**
Gladiator fights did not start as entertainment. They started at a funeral. In 264 BC, a powerful Roman named Brutus Pera died, and to honor him his sons held something new in the city's cattle market: armed men fighting in pairs. The Romans called it a munus, a duty owed to the dead. Soon rich families competed with bigger funeral games, and one funeral had 22 pairs. The games became a way to win the crowd. Centuries later, emperors filled giant arenas like the Colosseum, and a funeral ritual became Rome's most famous show.
