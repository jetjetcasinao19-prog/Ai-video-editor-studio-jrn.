- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Someone's making fun of you? Don't fight back. Do this."
- **Summary:** Therapists call it fogging. It comes from a 1975 book, *When I Say No, I
  Feel Guilty*. The trick: calmly agree with any part of the jab that's true. "Nice
  shoes, Grandpa." — "Yeah, they're comfy." "You're always late." — "True, I was late
  today." No anger, no defending. Like fog, they throw punches and nothing lands. It
  works because a tease wants a reaction; give it nothing and it has nothing to grab. One
  thing: this is for teasing. If it's bullying or it keeps happening, tell someone you
  trust.

- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Manuel J. Smith, *When I Say No, I Feel Guilty* (1975), a classic of
    assertiveness training. "Fogging" is one of its techniques: calmly agree with whatever
    part of a criticism is true (or could be true) without getting defensive or
    counter-attacking. Therapists and counselors still teach it as an assertiveness skill.
  - This is a communication technique, not a lab finding. There are no study numbers in
    the video. Say "can help," not "always works."
  - The example lines are illustrations. The shoes/late examples show agreeing only with
    the true part ("today," not "always").
  - Safety: the video says this is for teasing, and that bullying or teasing that keeps
    happening should be told to someone you trust. Keep that message in every caption
    and reply. Don't advise anyone to handle bullying alone.
