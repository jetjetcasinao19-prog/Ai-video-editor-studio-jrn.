- **Fact check (WebSearch summaries; I could not open any page in full, so re-check the wording before
  publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) Oath at age nine: a Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography entry (Perseus) places it at age nine,
  when Hamilcar took him to Spain, and says Hannibal told it to Antiochus years later, citing Polybius 3.11.
  Secondary summaries (History Herald, citing Mosig) say Hannibal told it at Antiochus III's court, and
  that its authenticity is unclear. Hence "as Hannibal later told it" in every caption. I did not read the
  Greek or English text of Polybius 3.11 itself, so I do not quote its wording.
  (2) Hamilcar Barca: Hannibal's father, a Carthaginian general (World History Encyclopedia, Britannica).
  (3) Born 247 BC (Britannica), so he was 29 in 218 BC. The card "AGE 9 → 29" holds (the birth year is a
  scholarly reckoning, so captions say "now a general", not "29").
  (4) 218 BC crossing of the Alps from Spain into Italy (Britannica, History.com).
  (5) Elephants: Polybius's figure of 37 is widely cited, and Britannica's Alpine crossing page says
  Hannibal descended into Italy with most of his 37 elephants still alive.
  (6) Cannae, 216 BC: Britannica says the Romans brought roughly 80,000 men against about 50,000 (40,000
  infantry and 10,000 cavalry); World History Encyclopedia gives over 80,000 vs under 50,000; Wikipedia
  says about 86,000. All ancient numbers are estimates.
  (7) Hannibal did not take Rome: historians debate why (no siege equipment, limited manpower, supplies).
