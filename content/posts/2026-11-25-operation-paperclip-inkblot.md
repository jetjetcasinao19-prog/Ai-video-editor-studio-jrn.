  - Sources: Annie Jacobsen, *Operation Paperclip* (2014); Linda Hunt, *Secret Agenda*

  - Operation Paperclip (1945–1959) brought more than 1,600 German scientists, engineers
    and technicians to the US. It was named for the paperclips attached to the files of
    those chosen.
  - Truman approved the program in 1946 on the condition that it exclude anyone who had
    been more than a nominal Nazi Party member or an active supporter of Nazism. JIOA
    officials rewrote or "bleached" security reports so that many could qualify anyway.
  - Wernher von Braun joined the Nazi Party in 1937 and the SS in 1940, reaching the rank
    of Sturmbannführer (an SS officer rank, roughly major). He later said membership was
    a formality; historians debate how much he knew and did, but he visited the
    Mittelwerk factory.
  - V-2 rockets were built in the underground Mittelwerk factory by prisoners of the
    Mittelbau-Dora camp. An estimated 20,000 prisoners died there — more people than
    the V-2 killed as a weapon. "Thousands died building them" is a cautious version.
  - Von Braun led the Marshall Space Flight Center team that developed the Saturn V,
    which launched Apollo 11 on July 16, 1969.
  - The halo effect: a positive impression in one area colors how we judge everything
    else about someone (Thorndike, 1920).
