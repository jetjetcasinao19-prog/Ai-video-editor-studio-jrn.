# One Word Makes People Say Yes — The Magic Word "Because", INKBLOT short

- **Video:** `animate/projects/because-copy-machine/out/because-copy-machine-inkblot.mp4`
  (64 s, 9:16, 1080x1920, @MINDTRACEOFFICIAL-YT mask logo, voice-over by the owner

  (YouTube Shorts allow up to 3 minutes).
- **Hook (first line of the video):** "One word makes people say yes, and it's not please."
- **Summary:** In 1978, psychologist Ellen Langer tested a small word at a busy copy
  machine. People asked to cut in line, three ways. "I have five pages, may I use the
  copy machine?" 60% said yes. "...because I'm in a rush?" 94% said yes. "...because I
  have to make copies?" (not a real reason, of course you do) still got 93%. The trick:
  your brain runs on autopilot. It hears "because", assumes a good reason is coming, and
  stops checking. The catch: for a bigger favor (20 pages), the empty "because" did
  nothing. Small favor? Say because. Big favor? Bring a real reason.
- **Edit notes:** a 2D animated short drawn entirely in code in the INKBLOT style
  (Rorschach-style living ink blots, psychology case-file look, red-pen notes).
  On-screen facts: "1978", "Ellen Langer", small request (5 pages): no reason 60%,
  real reason 94%, fake reason 93%; big request (20 pages): none 24%, real 42%,
  fake 24%. End card stamp "CASE: OPEN". Voice is the owner's own voice-over.
- **Audience:** United States (US English; times in content/schedule.md are in ET with Philippine time).
- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Langer, Blank & Chanowitz (1978), "The mindlessness of ostensibly
    thoughtful action: The role of 'placebic' information in interpersonal
    interaction", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. The numbers above match
    the paper: 5 pages: 60% (no reason), 94% (real reason), 93% (fake reason);
    20 pages: 24% (no reason), 42% (real reason), 24% (fake reason).
  - The study was done at a copy machine in a university setting. Do not name a
    specific university in any copy.
  - The voice-over says "strangers": these were the experimenters' confederates asking
    people who were already in line. Keep wording to "people in line" or "a busy copy
    machine" in captions; do not claim it was random passersby.
  - The "fake" reason ("because I have to make copies") adds no information, which is
    why the video calls it empty. Do not add other numbers, quotes or claims, and do
    not promise the trick works every time. Later research on mindlessness is mixed,
    so if asked, say that honestly and do not invent figures.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
