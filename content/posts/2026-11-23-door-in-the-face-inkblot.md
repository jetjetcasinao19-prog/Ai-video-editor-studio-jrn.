- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Manipulators don't start with what they want. They start with something crazy."
- **Summary:** In 1975, psychologist Robert Cialdini asked students to take kids from a
  juvenile detention center to the zoo. Only 17% said yes. Others were first asked
  something huge: volunteer two hours a week for two years. Everyone said no. Then,
  "Okay, could you just do the zoo trip?" Now 50% said yes. The trick: when they back
  down, you feel you should meet them halfway. So when a big ask suddenly shrinks, ask
  yourself: would I say yes to this one alone? (The opposite of the foot-in-the-door
  video: big first, then small.)
- **Edit notes:** a 2D animated short drawn entirely in code in the INKBLOT style
  (Rorschach-style living ink blots, psychology case-file look, red-pen notes).
  On-screen: an asker hiding a small card ("what they want") behind his back while
  opening with "A HUGE CRAZY ASK!"; "1975", "Robert Cialdini", a zoo gate with a
  giraffe, "about 2 hours, once"; a grid at 17%; a huge card "VOLUNTEER 2 HRS/WEEK FOR
  2 YEARS" with a tower of 104 weekly pages; a door slammed in the researcher's face
  ("NO", "door. in. the. face."); the door reopens and the big card shrinks into "JUST
  THE ZOO TRIP?" ("backing down…"); a grid from 17% to 50%; a bridge where one steps
  back and the other feels pulled to the middle ("halfway"); the small ask set alone
  under a spotlight with "Would I say yes to this one alone?". Voice is the owner's own
  voice-over.
- **Audience:** United States (US English; times in content/schedule.md are in ET with Philippine time).
- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Cialdini, R. B., Vincent, J. E., Lewis, S. K., Catalan, J., Wheeler, D., &
    Darby, B. L. (1975). "Reciprocal concessions procedure for inducing compliance: The
    door-in-the-face technique." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 31(2),
    206–215. Arizona State University; students were approached on campus.
  - Small request alone: chaperone a group of kids from the county juvenile detention
    center on a trip to the zoo (about two hours). 16.7% agreed (video rounds to 17%).
  - Large request first: serve as an unpaid counselor at the detention center two hours
    a week for at least two years. Nobody agreed. Asked right after for the zoo trip,
    50% agreed. The video's "everyone said no" and "50%" match Experiment 1.
  - "Meet them halfway" is the paper's explanation (reciprocal concessions). Later
    reviews find the effect is real but varies (it works best when the same person makes
    both requests soon after). Say "can," not "always."
  - "Manipulators" is the hook framing; the study used it for volunteering. The video
    explains the tactic so viewers can spot it, and ends with a check you can use.
  - The door, calendar tower, bridge and spotlight are illustrations. The INKBLOT look is
    a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
