- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Ferster, C. B., & Skinner, B. F. (1957). *Schedules of Reinforcement*.
    Appleton-Century-Crofts. Pigeons pecked a key for food under different schedules.
    After food stopped (extinction), birds that had been rewarded every time (continuous
    reinforcement) stopped sooner; birds on unpredictable (variable-ratio / variable-
    interval) schedules kept responding much longer. This is the "partial reinforcement
    extinction effect."
  - "Most addictive" in the voice-over is shorthand for "most resistant to extinction" —
    the hardest behavior to stop. The curves on screen are an illustration, not the
    study's numbers.
  - Slot machines pay out on a variable-ratio schedule (a standard textbook example).
  - Kind/cold cycles: psychologists use intermittent reinforcement to explain "trauma
    bonding" in abusive relationships (Dutton & Painter, 1981/1993). The video explains
    the pattern so people can recognize it; it doesn't teach it, and it isn't a diagnosis.
  - Pigeons in the 1950s: Skinner's pigeon work ran from the 1940s; the schedules book
    came out in 1957 (the case number on the cover).
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
