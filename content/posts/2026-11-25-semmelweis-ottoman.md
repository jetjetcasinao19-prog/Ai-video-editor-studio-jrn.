- **Hook (first line of the video):** "The doctor who told other doctors to wash their hands was rejected."
- **Summary:** In 1847 at Vienna General Hospital, Dr. Ignaz Semmelweis saw that mothers in the First Clinic (run by doctors) died of
  childbed fever far more often than mothers in the Second Clinic (run by midwives). Doctors often came to the ward straight from
  autopsies. In May 1847 he ordered handwashing in a chlorinated solution before examining patients, and deaths in the First Clinic fell
  sharply. Many doctors rejected his explanation. He published a book in 1861 and died in 1865, before germ theory won out in the
  decades after.

  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) Setting: Vienna General Hospital, First Clinic (doctors and medical students) versus Second Clinic (midwives); 1841–46 averages
  about 10% vs under 4% (one source; another gives 98.4 vs 36.2 per 1,000 births for 1840–46, about 9.8% vs 3.6%).
  (2) 1846 First Clinic annual rate 11.4% and 1848 rate 1.3%: Medical Journal of Australia, 2018.
  (3) Chlorine handwashing from about mid-May 1847, in chlorinated lime (the exact date is unknown). Trigger and context: autopsy
  doctors moving straight to the wards; colleague Jakob Kolletschka died after a cut during an autopsy (not used in the video).
  (4) Book: "Etiology, Concept and Prophylaxis of Childbed Fever," 1861.
  (5) Dates 1818–1865 (death 13 August 1865 per Wikipedia).

  Vienna hospital later dropped mandatory handwashing. Germ theory was widely accepted only in the 1870s and 1880s; Lister's
  antiseptic surgery dates to 1867.

**Title:** The Doctor Who Said "Wash Your Hands" Was Rejected #shorts
**Description:**
In 1847, Dr. Ignaz Semmelweis noticed that mothers in one Vienna hospital clinic, run by doctors, died of childbed fever far more often than in the clinic run by midwives. He ordered doctors to wash their hands in a chlorine solution before the ward, and deaths in the First Clinic fell sharply. Many doctors rejected his ideas, and he died in 1865, before germ theory won out.
