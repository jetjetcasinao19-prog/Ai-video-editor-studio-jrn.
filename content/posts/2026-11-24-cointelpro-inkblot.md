- **Fact check:**
  - Sources: US Senate Select Committee to Study Governmental Operations with Respect to
    Intelligence Activities (Church Committee), Final Report, Book III, "Dr. Martin Luther
    King, Jr., Case Study" and "COINTELPRO" (1976); Betty Medsger, *The Burglary* (2014).
  - The anonymous letter was mailed in November 1964 with a recording; it ended "There is
    only one thing left for you to do. You know what it is." King and those close to him
    read it as urging him to take his own life; the Church Committee published it. An
    unredacted copy was found in the National Archives in 2014.
  - COINTELPRO ran from 1956 to 1971 and targeted civil rights, anti-war, Black liberation
    and other groups (and the Communist Party, KKK and others). Tactics included anonymous
    and fake letters, planted rumors, and stirring conflict between and within groups.
  - On March 8, 1971, the Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI broke into the FBI's
    resident agency in Media, Pennsylvania, during the Muhammad Ali–Joe Frazier "Fight of
    the Century," expecting the noise to cover them. They mailed copies of the files to
    newspapers and members of Congress; The Washington Post published first. A file
    mentioning "COINTELPRO" led reporter Carl Stern to sue for more records.
  - "It stayed hidden until 1971" is shorthand: the full scope came out over 1971–1976.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
