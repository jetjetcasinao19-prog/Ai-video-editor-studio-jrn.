- **Summary:** Eyeglasses have no known inventor. Around 1306 a Dominican friar, Giordano da Pisa (also called Giordano da Rivalto), preached
  in Florence that the art of making glasses had been found less than twenty years earlier, and said he had met the man who made them. He never
  named him. Historians use that sermon, plus other evidence, to place the invention in northern Italy around the 1280s, with Pisa the
  favored city and Florence and Venice also proposed. The first spectacles were "rivet spectacles": two lenses on handles joined by a rivet.

- **Fact check (from WebSearch result summaries; I could not open any page in full, so open each link once before publishing):**
  Confirmed or well supported:
  (1) Giordano da Pisa (da Rivalto), a Dominican friar, preached in Florence; sources place the sermon at Santa Maria Novella. He said the
  art of making eyeglasses had been discovered "less than twenty years" earlier and that he had met the person who first made them. It is
  the earliest written mention of eyeglasses that historians cite.
  (2) The inventor is unnamed and unidentified.
  (3) Historians place the invention around the 1280s in northern Italy, partly from this sermon. Pisa is favored; Florence and Venice are also proposed.
  (4) Early spectacles were two convex lenses in rims joined at the handle ends by a rivet (a Venetian ordinance of 2 April 1300 describes
  this form).
  (5) Salvino degli Armati as inventor is considered a hoax: first mentioned in 1684, forgeries by Del Migliore, exposed by Isidoro del
  Lungo in 1920. Not used in the copy.
  (6) Pisa's tower: started 1173, already tilting after the first phase (to 1178), work resumed 1272 and stopped 1278 (Meloria), bell
  chamber finished 1372. So in the 1280s it was leaning and unfinished. The short tower on the map is accurate.
  (7) Florence's cathedral dome: built 1420-36 (Britannica). In 1306 there was no dome. Drawing a tower and baptistery without a dome is correct.
  Items to flag:
  (1) DATE. The "23 February 1306" date is not confirmed. One source gave 23 Feb 1306, others 1305, and one "1305 or 1306". My understanding
  (not checked in a source) is that 1305 is Florentine-calendar dating, since the Florentine year began on 25 March. The card "FLORENCE ·
  FEBRUARY 1306" and the narration "around 1306" are fine. The copy below says "around 1306" and "February" without a day number. Do not add "23 February".
  (2) PLACE. Santa Maria Novella is what the sources say, but the video only says "Florence". That is safe. The wooden pulpit is generic.
  (3) QUOTE. "NOT YET TWENTY YEARS..." is an English rendering of the Italian text, and the scroll reads as a paraphrase. The copy says "less
  than twenty years" and never presents it as a word-for-word quote.
  (4) "PROBABLY IN PISA OR FLORENCE" (narration). Sources say Pisa is favored but not certain, and Venice also has claims (earliest evidence
  of organized manufacture, 1300-1301). The narration is acceptable. The copy says "northern Italy, with Pisa often favored".
  (5) ALESSANDRO DELLA SPINA. A Dominican friar in Pisa who, according to a Pisan chronicle, made glasses after seeing them, and is not the
  inventor. Some scholars favor him as a candidate, which is a different claim from the chronicle. He is not shown in the video and is only
  mentioned carefully in the pinned comment. His 1317 epitaph is unreliable (the present one dates to 1841).
  (6) The Giordano sermon is only a clue. A museum curator says the passage is too thin to draw firm conclusions on its own. The copy says "clue" and "roughly".
