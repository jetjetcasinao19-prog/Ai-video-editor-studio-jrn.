- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Can't fall asleep because your brain won't stop? Do this."
- **Summary:** In 2018, Baylor University researchers brought people into a sleep lab.
  Before bed, half wrote a to-do list for tomorrow; half wrote what they'd already done.
  The to-do list group fell asleep about nine minutes faster. The trick: your brain keeps
  replaying unfinished tasks, and writing them down tells it they're handled. Five
  minutes. Pen and paper. Be specific.

- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Scullin, M. K., Krueger, M. L., Ballard, H. K., Pruett, N., & Bliwise, D. L.
    (2018). "The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep: A
    polysomnographic study comparing to-do lists and completed activity lists." *Journal
    of Experimental Psychology: General*, 147(1), 139–146. Baylor University (with Emory).
  - 57 young adults spent the night in a sleep lab. For 5 minutes before lights-out they
    handwrote either a to-do list for the next few days or a list of tasks they had
    completed. The to-do group fell asleep about 9 minutes faster (roughly 15 vs 25
    minutes), and more specific to-do lists went with falling asleep faster.
  - The bars on screen are an illustration of "about 9 minutes faster," not exact values.
  - "Your brain keeps replaying unfinished tasks" is the authors' explanation (offloading
    worries about unfinished tasks); it's a single, small study — say "can help," not
    "cures insomnia." Not medical advice; persistent sleep problems → see a doctor.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
