# Fix the Ending — The Peak-End Rule, INKBLOT short

- **Channel:** MindTrace (@MINDTRACEOFFICIAL-YT) — brand kit `brand/mindtrace/`
- **Video:** `animate/projects/peak-end-rule/out/peak-end-rule-inkblot.mp4`
  (38 s, 9:16, 1080x1920, voice-over by the owner (ElevenLabs voice "Bradford"),
  end card: MindTrace case-file cover (Case No. 1993), stamped "CASE: OPEN" and

- **Hook (first line of the video):** "Want people to remember you well? Fix the ending."
- **Summary:** In 1993, Daniel Kahneman had people hold a hand in painfully cold water
  for 60 seconds, then for 90 seconds, where the extra 30 were just slightly less cold.
  Asked which one they'd repeat, 69% chose the longer one — more pain! The trick: we
  judge an experience by its peak and its end. So end every date, talk and apology
  strong.
- **Edit notes:** a 2D animated short drawn entirely in code in the INKBLOT style
  (Rorschach-style living ink blots, psychology case-file look, red-pen notes).
  On-screen: a memory as a film strip whose last frame gets fixed ("the ending", "how
  they remember you"); "1993", "Daniel Kahneman", a hand in a bucket of icy water, a
  14 °C thermometer and a 60-second timer; a discomfort graph of trial A (60 s) vs trial
  B (90 s, the last 30 s slightly milder) as the thermometer creeps to 15 °C; cards A/B
  with "?"; a grid filling to 69% and a "MORE PAIN!" stamp; an experience curve with the
  peak and the end circled while the rest fades; date / talk / apology cards ending on an
  upward arrow and "END STRONG". Voice is the owner's own voice-over.
- **Audience:** United States (US English; times in content/schedule.md are in ET with Philippine time).
- **Fact check:**
  - Source: Kahneman, D., Fredrickson, B. L., Schreiber, C. A., & Redelmeier, D. A.
    (1993). "When more pain is preferred to less: Adding a better end." *Psychological
    Science*, 4(6), 401–405.
  - The short trial was 60 s with a hand in 14 °C water. The long trial was the same
    60 s followed by 30 s more while the water was warmed slightly to about 15 °C
    (still painful, a bit less so).
  - Asked which trial they would repeat, 69% (22 of 32) chose the longer one.
  - "Peak-end rule": we remember an experience mostly by its most intense moment and how
    it ended, and largely ignore its length (duration neglect). Well replicated (e.g.
    Redelmeier & Kahneman's colonoscopy study), though not universal.
  - "End every date, talk and apology strong" is the takeaway, not something the study
    tested. The graph and film strip are illustrations, not the study's data.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
