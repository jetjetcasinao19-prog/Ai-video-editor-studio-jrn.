- **Fact check:**
  - Sources: Haney, C., Banks, C., & Zimbardo, P. (1973). "Interpersonal dynamics in a
    simulated prison." *International Journal of Criminology and Penology*, 1, 69–97;
    Le Texier, T. (2019). "Debunking the Stanford Prison Experiment." *American
    Psychologist*, 74(7), 823–839; Blum, B. (2018), "The Lifespan of a Lie," *Medium*.
  - August 1971, in the basement of Stanford's psychology building; funded by the US
    Office of Naval Research. 24 male college students were randomly assigned to be
    guards or prisoners. Planned for two weeks, it was stopped after six days.
  - Le Texier's archive research found recordings and documents showing the
    experimenters (including the "warden") encouraging guards to be tough, and guards
    who said they were acting out what they thought was wanted. Douglas Korpi, the
    prisoner whose breakdown became famous, said in 2017 that he had faked it to get out
    (he wanted to study for exams). Zimbardo disputed these critiques; he died in 2024.
  - "Partly staged" is the critics' view, not a court finding; say "critics say" if
    challenged. Critics also point out that the study had no control group.
  - "Tell people what you expect and they'll give it to you" describes demand
    characteristics (Orne, 1962): participants pick up on what researchers want and act
    it out.
  - The INKBLOT look is a style choice, not a claim about Rorschach or a psychological test.
