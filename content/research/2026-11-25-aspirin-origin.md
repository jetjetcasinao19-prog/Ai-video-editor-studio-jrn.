# Research — The origin of aspirin (from willow bark to a lab)

Topic type: Inventions & discoveries (lab / medicine) / Businesses
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-25

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Willow bark was used as medicine for pain and fever in ancient times; Hippocrates described it around 400 BC | [DPMA (German Patent Office)](https://www.dpma.de/en/explore/milestones/brands-with-history/aspirin), [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/aspirin/) |
| In 1828, Munich professor Johann Büchner isolated salicin from willow | [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/aspirin/), [Wikipedia — History of aspirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_aspirin) |
| In 1897 at Bayer (Germany), acetylsalicylic acid was synthesized in usable form; credit traditionally goes to Felix Hoffmann, but his boss Arthur Eichengrün later claimed he directed the work | [DPMA](https://www.dpma.de/en/explore/milestones/brands-with-history/aspirin), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/aspirin-invention-controversy), [PMC / BMJ article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1119266/) |
| On March 6, 1899, Bayer registered the trade name Aspirin | [DPMA](https://www.dpma.de/en/explore/milestones/brands-with-history/aspirin), [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/aspirin/) |
| Historians still debate who deserves credit (Hoffmann vs. Eichengrün, who was Jewish and was left out of a 1934 account) | [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/aspirin-invention-controversy), [PMC / BMJ article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1119266/) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Claims about ancient Egyptians/Sumerians using willow: loosely sourced; not used.
- An earlier synthesis by Charles Gerhardt (1853) existed: left out for length; script says Bayer "makes a version" rather than "invents it from nothing".
- Where the name "Aspirin" comes from (Spirsäure / Spiraea): not fully confirmed; not used.
- The story that Hoffmann did it for his father's arthritis: disputed; not used.
