# Research — The origin of "laconic" (the Spartans' one-word reply)

Topic type: Ancient history (war) / Words
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-24

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| "Laconic" comes from Laconia, the region of Greece that included Sparta; Spartans were known for very short, blunt speech | [Wikipedia — Laconic phrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laconic_phrase), [Greek Reporter](https://greekreporter.com/2025/04/22/spartan-wit-history-sharpest-one-liner/) |
| As told by Plutarch, Philip II of Macedon threatened that if he invaded Laconia he would destroy them (their farms, people and city); the Spartans replied with one word: "If" | [Wikipedia — Laconic phrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laconic_phrase), [Greek Reporter](https://greekreporter.com/2025/04/22/spartan-wit-history-sharpest-one-liner/), [We Are The Mighty](https://www.wearethemighty.com/mighty-culture/ancient-spartan-retorts/) |
| Philip II was the father of Alexander the Great | [Wikipedia — Laconic phrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laconic_phrase) (context), widely documented |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- The exact wording of Philip's threat varies by retelling: script paraphrases ("destroy your city").
- An earlier part of the exchange ("as friend or enemy?" — "Neither") and what happened afterward: left out for length.
- The story comes from Plutarch, centuries later: script says "as the story goes".
