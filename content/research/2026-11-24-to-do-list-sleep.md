# Research — Can't sleep? Write a to-do list

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("what to do when…" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Scullin, M. K., Krueger, M. L., Ballard, H. K., Pruett, N., & Bliwise, D. L.
  (2018). "The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep: A
  polysomnographic study comparing to-do lists and completed activity lists."
  *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 147(1), 139–146.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000374
- Baylor University sleep lab; 57 healthy young adults, sleep measured with
  lab equipment (polysomnography), on a weeknight.
- For 5 minutes before lights-out, people were randomly assigned to write
  either a to-do list for the next few days or a list of tasks they had
  already completed.
- The to-do list group fell asleep about 9 minutes faster on average.
- The more specific the to-do list, the faster people fell asleep.

## Safe to say
- "About 9 minutes faster" in this study. Don't say it cures insomnia.
- Explanation (the authors' idea): writing tasks down may "offload" the
  worry about unfinished tasks.
