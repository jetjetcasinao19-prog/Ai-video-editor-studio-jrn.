# Research — How Roman gladiator fights began

Topic type: Ancient history (war / Rome)
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-23

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| The first recorded gladiator fights in Rome were in 264 BC, held by the sons of Junius Brutus Pera at his funeral, in the Forum Boarium (the cattle market) | [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/gladiator/), [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/first-roman-gladiators), [Encyclopaedia Romana (U. Chicago)](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_romana/gladiators/gladiators.html) |
| Several pairs of armed men fought (often given as three pairs) | [Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_romana/gladiators/gladiators.html), [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/gladiator/) |
| The games were called "munera" (sing. munus): a duty/offering owed to the dead | [Spectacles in the Roman World sourcebook](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/spectaclesintheromanworldsourcebook/chapter/development-of-the-munera/), [Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_romana/gladiators/gladiators.html) |
| The fighters at that funeral are described as prisoners | [Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_romana/gladiators/gladiators.html), [Royal Armouries](https://royalarmouries.org/the-world-of-the-gladiators) |
| Funeral games grew: 22 pairs at the funeral of Marcus Aemilius Lepidus (216 BC) | [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/gladiator/), [Wikipedia — Gladiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladiator) |
| The games became a way for politicians to win public favor; later, emperors staged them in big arenas such as the Colosseum | [History.co.uk](https://history.co.uk/articles/gladiatorial-games-in-ancient-rome), [Royal Armouries](https://royalarmouries.org/the-world-of-the-gladiators), [Wikipedia — Gladiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladiator) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Number of fighters in 264 BC (three pairs vs. other counts): script says "armed men fighting in pairs".
- Whether the fighters were prisoners of war: one source says prisoners; script says "likely prisoners of war".
- Origin outside Rome (Campania/Etruria): left out for length.
- The "thumbs down" gesture: unclear in sources; not used.
