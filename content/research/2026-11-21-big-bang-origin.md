# Research — The origin of the Big Bang theory (and its name)

Topic type: Theory / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-21

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1927, Georges Lemaître, a Belgian Catholic priest and physicist, published a solution showing an expanding universe; it went largely unnoticed at first | [AMNH](https://www.amnh.org/learn-teach/curriculum-collections/cosmic-horizons/profile-georges-lemaitre-father-of-the-big-bang), [APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2026/05/lemaitre-proposes-universe-beginning), [Wikipedia — Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lema%C3%AEtre) |
| In 1931 he proposed that the universe began from a single "primeval atom" | [AMNH](https://www.amnh.org/learn-teach/curriculum-collections/cosmic-horizons/profile-georges-lemaitre-father-of-the-big-bang), [Wikipedia — History of the Big Bang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Big_Bang_theory) |
| Einstein at first rejected the expanding universe; he is reported to have told Lemaître (1927) his calculations were correct but his physics "abominable" | [Wikipedia — Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lema%C3%AEtre), [AMNH](https://www.amnh.org/learn-teach/curriculum-collections/cosmic-horizons/profile-georges-lemaitre-father-of-the-big-bang) |
| Fred Hoyle, who backed the rival Steady State theory (a universe with no beginning), said "this 'big bang' idea" on BBC radio on March 28, 1949; the name stuck | [Wikipedia — History of the Big Bang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Big_Bang_theory), [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/lemaitre-proposes-big-bang-theory) |
| In 1965, Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson (US) reported the cosmic microwave background, leftover heat from the early universe | [Nobel Prize 1978](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1978/summary/), [Wikipedia — History of the Big Bang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Big_Bang_theory) |
| Lemaître learned of the discovery shortly before his death (1966) | [Wikipedia — Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lema%C3%AEtre) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Whether Hoyle meant "big bang" as an insult (he later said he didn't): script only quotes what he said and that he doubted the idea.
- Friedmann (1922/1924) and Gamow also shaped the theory: left out for length; script doesn't call Lemaître the only founder.
- The Einstein quote is reported second-hand: script says "reportedly".
