# Research — The invention of the stethoscope

Topic type: Inventions (medicine) / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-24

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1816, in Paris, French doctor René Laennec examined a young woman with heart symptoms | [Wikipedia — René Laennec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Laennec), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/laennec-invents-stethoscope), [Linda Hall Library](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/rene-theophile-hyacinthe-laennec) |
| Doctors then listened by placing an ear directly on the chest; Laennec felt this was improper for this patient | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/rene-laennec), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/laennec-invents-stethoscope) |
| He rolled paper into a tube, pressed one end to her chest, and heard the heart more clearly than before (his own account) | [Wikipedia — René Laennec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Laennec), [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/rene-laennec) |
| He then made wooden versions and named it the stethoscope, from Greek stethos (chest) and skopos (examination) | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/rene-laennec), [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp37490/rene-theophile-hyacinthe-laennec), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/327655) |
| He described it in his 1819 book on listening to the chest (De l'auscultation médiate) | [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/laennec-invents-stethoscope), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/327655) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Story of children tapping a log as his inspiration: not confirmed; not used.
- One source dates the invention to 1819 (the book year); most say 1816: script uses 1816.
- Patient described as having a heavy build (making tapping/feeling hard): left out for length.
