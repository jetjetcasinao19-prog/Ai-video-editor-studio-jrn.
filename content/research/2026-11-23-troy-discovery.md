## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| British official Frank Calvert believed Troy lay under the mound of Hisarlik (in today's Turkey), dug there before Schliemann, and suggested Schliemann dig there | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2196/discovery-of-troy/), [Wikipedia — Frank Calvert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Calvert), [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/how-heinrich-schliemann-rediscovered-troyand-then-destroyed-it) |
| Heinrich Schliemann, a wealthy German businessman, began digging at Hisarlik in 1870 | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2196/discovery-of-troy/), [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/excavation-ancient-troy) |
| He dug a huge trench (about 45 ft / 14 m) through the mound, throwing aside layers he thought too late, destroying parts of the layers linked to Homer's Troy | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2196/discovery-of-troy/), [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/how-heinrich-schliemann-rediscovered-troyand-then-destroyed-it), [Wikipedia — Heinrich Schliemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Schliemann) |
| In 1873 he announced "Priam's Treasure"; it came from the Troy II layer, about 1,000 years older than Homer's Troy | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2196/discovery-of-troy/), [Wikipedia — Priam's Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priam%27s_Treasure) |
| He smuggled the treasure out of the Ottoman Empire; the Ottomans sued and he paid compensation | [Wikipedia — Priam's Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priam%27s_Treasure), [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2196/discovery-of-troy/) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- First dig year (1870) vs. main campaign (1871–73): script says 1870.
- New claim (Nat Geo, 2026) that he mixed artifacts from different layers for publicity: not settled; not used.
- Whether the Trojan War itself happened as Homer tells it: script only says people thought it was "just a myth" until the site was found.
- His wife Sophia carrying the gold in her shawl: his story, shown false; not used.
