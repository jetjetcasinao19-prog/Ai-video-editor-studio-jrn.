# Research — Forgot why you walked into a room? The doorway effect

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("what to do when…" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Radvansky, G. A., Krawietz, S. A., & Tamplin, A. K. (2011). "Walking
  through doorways causes forgetting: Further explorations." *Quarterly
  Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 64(8), 1632–1645.
  https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2011.571267
- University of Notre Dame. People picked up and moved objects from table to
  table, either in a virtual (video-game style) environment or in real rooms.
  Memory for the object they were carrying was worse after walking through a
  doorway than after walking the same distance within one room.
- Going back to the original room did not bring the memory back in their
  tests (so "go back to the room" is not supported by this study).
- Explanation (event model / "event boundary"): the brain treats a doorway as
  the end of one event and the start of another, and files the old one away.

## Caution
- Later replications are mixed: e.g., McFadyen, J., et al. (2021), *Journal of
  Experimental Psychology: General*, found the effect mainly when people were
  juggling a lot in memory. Say "made them forget more" in this study; don't
  say it happens every time.

## The tip (advice, not tested here)
- Say what you're going for out loud before you go (puts it in words, a
  common memory strategy).
