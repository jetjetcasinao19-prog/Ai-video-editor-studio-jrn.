# Research — Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, the first person to see bacteria

Topic type: Discoveries (lab / science) / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-25

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Antonie van Leeuwenhoek of Delft (Netherlands) was not a trained scientist; he worked in the cloth trade (apprenticed to a draper) | [UC Berkeley Museum of Paleontology](https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/history/leeuwenhoek.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek), [TU Delft](https://tudelft.nl/en/antoni-van-leeuwenhoekjaar/antoni-van-leeuwenhoek) |
| He ground his own lenses and built hundreds of single-lens microscopes (he did not invent the microscope) | [UC Berkeley](https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/history/leeuwenhoek.html), [FSU Molecular Expressions](https://micro.magnet.fsu.edu/optics/timeline/people/leeuwenhoek.html) |
| In water samples (1670s) he saw tiny moving creatures he called "animalcules"; he is credited with first seeing bacteria | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek/), [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10509104), [UC Berkeley](https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/history/leeuwenhoek.html) |
| On Sept 17, 1683, he described many tiny living animalcules in the plaque from his own teeth | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek), [University of Houston](https://grants.hhp.uh.edu/clayne/HistoryofMC/HistoryMC/Leeuwenhoek.htm) |
| He reported his findings in letters to the Royal Society of London for about 50 years | [UC Berkeley](https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/history/leeuwenhoek.html), [Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Antonie-van-Leeuwenhoek/47611) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out

- Exact year of the first bacteria sighting (1674–1676): script says "the 1670s".
