# Research — Project Stargate (US "psychic spies")

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Sources
- Mumford, M. D., Rose, A. M., & Goslin, D. A. (1995). *An Evaluation of
  Remote Viewing: Research and Applications.* American Institutes for
  Research (AIR), prepared for the CIA. Reviewers included statistician
  Jessica Utts and psychologist Ray Hyman. Available in the CIA FOIA Reading
  Room (search "Stargate"): https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/
- CIA Reading Room collection "STAR GATE [Controlled Remote Viewing]".

## Facts (safe to say)
- From the early 1970s to 1995, US government agencies (CIA research at the
  Stanford Research Institute from 1972; Army and Defense Intelligence Agency
  units from 1978) funded "remote viewing": trying to describe distant or
  hidden targets with the mind. The unit was at Fort Meade, Maryland. The
  name "Stargate" was used from about 1991. Over 20 years in total.
- Total cost commonly reported: about $20 million.
- The 1995 AIR evaluation concluded remote viewing had never been used to
  guide any intelligence operation; the information was vague and ambiguous
  and needed a lot of interpretation. The program was ended in 1995.
- (Utts argued lab results were statistically interesting; Hyman disagreed.
  The script only uses the agreed conclusion: no operational value.)

## The psychology (the "trick")
- Forer, B. R. (1949). "The fallacy of personal validation: A classroom
  demonstration of gullibility." *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology*,
  44(1), 118–123. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0059240
- Students got the same vague personality description, presented as personal;
  most rated it as very accurate. Called the Forer or Barnum effect: vague
  statements feel personal and accurate because people fill in the details.
- Linking Stargate's vague reports to this effect is an explanation, not a
  finding of the AIR report: say "vague feels accurate".
