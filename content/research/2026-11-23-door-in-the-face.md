# Research — Door in the face (a manipulation tactic)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: under-60 s YouTube Short.
Angle: how manipulation works so the viewer can spot it (defensive).

## The study
- Cialdini, R. B., Vincent, J. E., Lewis, S. K., Catalan, J., Wheeler, D., &
  Darby, B. L. (1975). "Reciprocal concessions procedure for inducing
  compliance: The door-in-the-face technique." *Journal of Personality and
  Social Psychology*, 31(2), 206–215. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076284
- Experiment 1, Arizona State University: students walking on campus were
  asked to chaperone a group of juvenile delinquents (teens from a county
  juvenile detention center) on a two-hour trip to the zoo.
  - Asked only the zoo request: about 17% (16.7%) agreed.
  - Asked first to be an unpaid counselor for the juvenile detention center,
    two hours a week for at least two years (no one agreed), then asked the
    zoo request: 50% agreed.
- Explanation (the authors): reciprocal concessions. The asker "backs down",
  so the target feels pressure to back down too and meet them halfway.

## Safe framing
- Cialdini studied how people get compliance; the "manipulators" line is the
  hook, and the script's job is to help viewers spot the move.
- Defense (general advice): judge the smaller request on its own:
  "Would I say yes to this if it came first?"
- Don't say it always works; say what happened in this study.
