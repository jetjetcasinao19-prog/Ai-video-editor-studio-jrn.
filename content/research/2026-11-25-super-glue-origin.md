# Research — The invention of Super Glue

Topic type: Inventions (lab) / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-25

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1942, during World War II, Kodak chemist Harry Coover was trying to make a clear plastic for precision gun sights from cyanoacrylates | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/harry-coover), [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/technology/super-glue-invention-mistake/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Coover) |
| The material stuck permanently to everything, so it was set aside (too sticky) | [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/technology/super-glue-invention-mistake/), [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/harry-coover), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/almanac-superglue-harry-coover) |
| Years later (1951), a colleague tested formulation "910" between the prisms of a lab instrument (a refractometer) and glued them together; Coover realized it was a useful adhesive | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Coover), [Today I Found Out](https://www.todayifoundout.com/?p=62179) |
| It went on sale in 1958 as "Eastman #910" | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Coover), [Make Tech Easier](https://maketecheasier.com/super-glue-harry-coover-eastman-910/) |
| Reportedly used as a spray to seal wounds in the Vietnam War | [Wikipedia — Cyanoacrylate / Superglue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanoacrylate), [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/off-duty/military-culture/2023/11/24/how-world-war-ii-necessitated-the-invention-of-super-glue/) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- The 1951 refractometer story comes from fewer sources: script says "years later… it glues expensive lab equipment together".
- Vietnam War wound use: "reportedly"; moved to the pinned comment.
- Coover was not the only chemist involved (team work at Kodak): script says "his team" for the rediscovery.
