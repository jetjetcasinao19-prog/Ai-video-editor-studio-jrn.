# Research — Craving a snack? Imagine eating it (a lot)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("what to do when…" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Morewedge, C. K., Huh, Y. E., & Vosgerau, J. (2010). "Thought for food:
  Imagined consumption reduces actual consumption." *Science*, 330(6010),
  1530–1533. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1195701
- Carnegie Mellon University. People imagined eating M&M's one at a time
  (30 or 3), or imagined a different repetitive action (e.g., putting coins
  into a laundry machine), then could eat from a real bowl of M&M's.
- People who imagined eating 30 M&M's ate fewer real M&M's than those who
  imagined eating 3 or imagined the other action.
- Imagining eating only 3 did not reduce eating.
- The effect was food-specific: imagining eating M&M's reduced M&M eating,
  not cheese eating (and vice versa). The authors' explanation: habituation,
  the brain "getting used to" a food through repeated imagined bites.

## Caution
- Later replication attempts have had mixed results. Say "in this study";
  don't promise it kills every craving. No numbers in the script.
- Brand: saying "M&M's" is fine; don't show the logo or packaging.
