# Research — The "because" trick (placebic reasons)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: 60 s YouTube Short.

## The study
- Langer, E., Blank, A., & Chanowitz, B. (1978). "The mindlessness of
  ostensibly thoughtful action: The role of 'placebic' information in
  interpersonal interaction." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*,
  36(6), 635–642. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.36.6.635
- Setup: a stranger asked people who were about to use a copy machine if they
  could use it first. Three ways of asking:
  1. Request only: "Excuse me, I have 5 pages. May I use the Xerox machine?"
  2. Real reason: "... because I'm in a rush?"
  3. Placebic (empty) reason: "... because I have to make copies?"

## Results (share who let the stranger go first)
| Request | No reason | Empty reason | Real reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (5 pages) | 60% | 93% | 94% |
| Large (20 pages) | 24% | 24% | 42% |

## What it means (safe to say)
- For a small ask, the word "because" plus *any* reason worked almost as well
  as a real reason. Langer's explanation: people respond "mindlessly", on
  autopilot, to the structure of a request.
- For a big ask, the empty reason did nothing; only the real reason helped.
- Don't say "it always works" or "100%". Say it worked for small favors in
  this study.
