# Research — One word rewrites memory (Loftus & Palmer)

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series: how memory can be manipulated).
Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Loftus, E. F., & Palmer, J. C. (1974). "Reconstruction of automobile
  destruction: An example of the interaction between language and memory."
  *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 13(5), 585–589.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(74)80011-3
- Experiment 1: students watched films of car crashes, then were asked "About
  how fast were the cars going when they ___ each other?" Average speed
  estimates: "smashed" 40.8 mph, "collided" 39.3, "bumped" 38.1, "hit" 34.0,
  "contacted" 31.8.
- Experiment 2: 150 students saw a film of a crash. Some were asked the
  "smashed" question, some the "hit" question, some no speed question.
  A week later they were asked "Did you see any broken glass?" There was no
  broken glass in the film.
  - "Smashed": 32% (16 of 50) said yes.
  - "Hit": 14% (7 of 50) said yes.
  - No speed question: 12% (6 of 50) said yes.

## Safe framing
- The study shows wording can change memory; the link to gaslighting is an
  application, so say "manipulators use the same weakness", not "the study
  was about gaslighting".
- "Write it down" is general advice, not tested here.
