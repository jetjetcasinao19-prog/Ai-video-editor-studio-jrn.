# Research — The origin of the phrase "Pyrrhic victory"

Topic type: Ancient history (war) / Words
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-23

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| The term comes from King Pyrrhus of Epirus (in northwestern Greece), who fought Rome in the Pyrrhic War | [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Pyrrhic-victory), [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Pyrrhus/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhic_victory) |
| In 280 BC he went to Italy (helping the Greek city of Tarentum against Rome) with war elephants (about 20) | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Pyrrhus/), [WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/ancient-world/who-was-pyrrhus-and-what-is-a-pyrrhic-victory.html), [TheCollector](https://www.thecollector.com/what-is-a-pyrrhic-victory/) |
| He defeated Rome at Heraclea (280 BC) and at Asculum (279 BC), but with heavy, hard-to-replace losses, including many officers | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhic_victory), [History Skills](https://historyskills.com/classroom/ancient-history/pyrrhic-victory), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/5-famous-pyrrhic-victories) |
| Plutarch reports that, when congratulated, Pyrrhus said one more such victory would ruin him | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhic_victory), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Pyrrhic-victory) |
| After the battle of Beneventum (275 BC) he withdrew from Italy | [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Pyrrhus/), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/pyrrhus) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Exact wording of the quote varies by translation, and Plutarch is the only ancient source: script says "reportedly" and paraphrases.
- Casualty numbers: not used.
- Whether the remark followed Heraclea or Asculum: script places it after the wins without naming which.
