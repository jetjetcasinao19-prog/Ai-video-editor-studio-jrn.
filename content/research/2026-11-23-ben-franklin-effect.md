# Research — The Ben Franklin effect

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: under-60 s YouTube Short.

## The story (primary source)
- Benjamin Franklin, *Autobiography* (written 1771–1790). Public domain text:
  https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20203
- When Franklin was chosen again as clerk of the Pennsylvania General Assembly
  (the 1730s; he was first chosen in 1736), a new member made a long speech
  against him.
- Franklin did not try to win him with "servile respect". He heard the man had
  "a very scarce and curious book" in his library, wrote asking to borrow it
  for a few days, and the man sent it right away.
- Franklin returned it about a week later with a note thanking him.
- Next time they met in the House, the man spoke to Franklin ("which he had
  never done before") with great civility, was ready to help him after that,
  and "we became great friends, and our friendship continued to his death."
- Franklin's maxim: "He that has once done you a kindness will be more ready
  to do you another, than he whom you yourself have obliged."

## The study
- Jecker, J., & Landy, D. (1969). "Liking a person as a function of doing him
  a favour." *Human Relations*, 22(4), 371–378.
  https://doi.org/10.1177/001872676902200407
- Participants won money in a task. Afterward, some were asked by the
  researcher, as a personal favor, to give the money back (he said it was his
  own money). Others were asked by a secretary (department funds) or not asked.
- Those who did the researcher the personal favor rated him as more likable.
- Small study; later work supports the effect but it is not huge. Don't give
  numbers or say it always works.

## Explanation (safe to say)
- Common explanation: cognitive dissonance / self-perception ("I helped him,
  so I must like him").
