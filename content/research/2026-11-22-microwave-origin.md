# Research — The origin of the microwave oven

Topic type: Inventions / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-22

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Percy Spencer, a Raytheon engineer in Massachusetts, was largely self-taught (no formal education beyond grammar school) | [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/microwave-oven-invention), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer), [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer) |
| While working near a magnetron (the tube that makes microwaves for radar), he noticed a candy/chocolate bar in his pocket had melted | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer), [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-history-of-the-microwave-oven), [APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/10/first-patent-for-microwave) |
| He experimented further; an egg test ended with the egg bursting | [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/microwave-oven-invention) |
| Raytheon filed a patent on October 8, 1945 | [APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/10/first-patent-for-microwave), [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer) |
| The first commercial microwave oven, the Radarange, was about 6 feet tall and weighed about 750 pounds | [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/microwave-oven-invention), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer), [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer) |
| Spencer received only Raytheon's standard $2 bonus for patents | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/microwave-oven-invention) |

## Points sources disagree on (kept out of the script)
- Radarange launch year (1946 vs. 1947) and price ($3,000 vs. $5,000): script gives neither.
- Popcorn as an early test: not confirmed here; script uses the egg.
- Number of his patents (100+ vs. 300): not used.
