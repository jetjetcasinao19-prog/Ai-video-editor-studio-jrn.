# Research — The origin of "Taps"

Topic type: History (war — Civil War)
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-23

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| The story of Capt. Robert Ellicombe finding the music on his dead son is a legend, not history | [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/music/songs/taps.asp), [American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/magazine/2012/october/all-is-well-safely-rest), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/how-did-taps-originate) |
| In July 1862, Union Gen. Daniel Butterfield's brigade was camped at Harrison's Landing, Virginia, after the Seven Days Battles | [Arlington National Cemetery (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121019175436/http:/www.arlingtoncemetery.mil/Events/Traditions/Taps.aspx), [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/82706/birthplace_of_taps_celebrates_bugle_calls_150th_anniversary), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/how-did-taps-originate) |
| Butterfield reworked an earlier call (an early "Tattoo", linked to the "Scott Tattoo") with brigade bugler Oliver W. Norton, who first sounded it; other units soon copied it | [U.S. Marine Band](https://www.marineband.marines.mil/Audio-Resources/Taps), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taps_(bugle_call)), [American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/magazine/2012/october/all-is-well-safely-rest) |
| Its official name in manuals was "Extinguish Lights" until 1891; "Taps" was the soldiers' name | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taps_(bugle_call)), [AUSA](https://www.ausa.org/history-taps) |
| It came to be played at military funerals and became a standard part of them in 1891 | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taps_(bugle_call)), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/deceptivecadence/2012/05/18/152939191/150-years-of-taps) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Details differ between Norton's 1898 account and Butterfield's reply: script keeps only the parts both agree on.
- Who first used it at a funeral (Capt. John Tidball's battery is often credited): left out for length.
