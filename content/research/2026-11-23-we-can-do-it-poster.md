# Research — The origin of the "We Can Do It!" (Rosie the Riveter) poster

Topic type: History (war — World War II)
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-23

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Artist J. Howard Miller made the "We Can Do It!" poster in 1943 for Westinghouse Electric to boost worker morale | [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/highlights/we-can-do-it), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can_Do_It!), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/We-Can-Do-It) |
| It was only used internally, shown for about two weeks in February 1943 in some Westinghouse factories, then disappeared for decades | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can_Do_It!), [UW iSchool](https://ischool.uw.edu/podcasts/dtctw/we-can-do-it-poster), [Poster House](https://posterhouse.org/blog/hot-poster-gossip-rosie-edition/) |
| During the war, "Rosie the Riveter" meant the 1942 song and Norman Rockwell's widely seen painting, not this poster | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can_Do_It!), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/rori/learn/historyculture/who-was-rosie-the-riveter.htm), [Poster House](https://posterhouse.org/blog/hot-poster-gossip-rosie-edition/) |
| It was rediscovered and widely reproduced in the 1980s, became linked to the name "Rosie the Riveter", and became a symbol of women's strength / empowerment | [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/rori/learn/historyculture/who-was-rosie-the-riveter.htm), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can_Do_It!), [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/highlights/we-can-do-it) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Who the poster's model was (Naomi Parker Fraley per Prof. James Kimble's research): not fully confirmed in these results; not used.
- Exact number of factories: script says "a few".
