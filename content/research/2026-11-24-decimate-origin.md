# Research — The origin of the word "decimate"

Topic type: Ancient history (war) / Words
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-24

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| "Decimation" comes from Latin decimatio, "removal of a tenth" | [Wikipedia — Decimation (punishment)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimation_(punishment)), [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/decimation/) |
| It was the Roman army's most extreme punishment for a unit that had disgraced itself (e.g., cowardice or mutiny) | [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/decimation/), [History of War](https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/concepts_decimate.html) |
| Soldiers were divided into groups of ten and drew lots; the one chosen was killed by the other members of the unit | [Wikipedia — Decimation (punishment)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimation_(punishment)), [UNRV](https://unrv.com/military/decimation.php), [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/decimation/) |
| It was used rarely, because it was so harsh | [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/decimation/), [Cambridge — "Decimatio: myth, discipline and death"](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/504D6242E0621DE1B2CC0D32B92765E3/S0066477422000090a.pdf/decimatio-myth-discipline-and-death-in-the-roman-republic.pdf) |
| Today "decimate" usually means to destroy most of something | [GPB / NPR](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/05/01/decimate-means-much-more-today-it-did-in-ancient-rome), [Wikipedia — Decimation (punishment)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimation_(punishment)) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Crassus's use against his own troops during the Spartacus revolt (72 or 71 BC; numbers exaggerated in Appian): left out for length.
- Exact method of execution (clubbing, stoning): not used; script says "killed by the other nine".
