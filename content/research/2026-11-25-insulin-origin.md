# Research — The discovery of insulin and the $1 patent

Topic type: Inventions & discoveries (lab / medicine) / People
Audience: US viewers, 16–34 (November is American Diabetes Month)
Date: 2026-11-25

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Before insulin, diabetes (type 1) was effectively a death sentence | [Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/the-miracle-discovery-that-reversed-the-diabetes-death-sentence/), [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/the-discovery-of-insulin) |
| In 1921, Frederick Banting and medical student Charles Best began experiments at the University of Toronto with a pancreatic extract | [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/the-discovery-of-insulin), [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/how-a-medical-student-helped-discover-lifesaving-insulin) |
| James Collip purified the extract; John Macleod led the lab — the discovery was teamwork | [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/the-discovery-of-insulin), [CMAJ](https://www.cmaj.ca/content/193/42/E1636), [University of Toronto Libraries](https://fisherdigitus.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/insulin100/from-embarrassment-to-triumph) |
| In January 1922, teenager Leonard Thompson (age 13 or 14) received the first injections at Toronto General Hospital; after a failed first dose, a purified extract worked dramatically | [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/the-discovery-of-insulin), [University of Toronto Libraries](https://fisherdigitus.library.utoronto.ca/exhibits/show/insulin100/from-embarrassment-to-triumph) |
| Banting, Best and Collip sold their US patents to the University of Toronto for $1 each | [Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/the-miracle-discovery-that-reversed-the-diabetes-death-sentence/) |
| Banting and Macleod won the 1923 Nobel Prize; they shared their prize money with Best and Collip | [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/the-discovery-of-insulin), [Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/the-miracle-discovery-that-reversed-the-diabetes-death-sentence/) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Thompson's age (13 vs. 14): script says "a teenage boy".
- The $1 patent sale comes from one source in this research (the Nobel Prize site): kept, since it is the official Nobel source.
- The quote often given as "Insulin belongs to the world": not confirmed; not used.
