# Research — Milgram's obedience study ("mind control")

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: under-60 s YouTube Short.
Angle: "the closest thing to real mind control" = obedience to authority.
(There is no real mind control; the script uses the phrase as a hook only.)

## The study
- Milgram, S. (1963). "Behavioral study of obedience." *Journal of Abnormal
  and Social Psychology*, 67(4), 371–378. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0040525
- Run at Yale University (experiments began in 1961). 40 men, ages 20–50,
  from the New Haven area, paid to take part in a "learning" study.
- The participant ("teacher") was told to give the "learner" an electric
  shock for each wrong answer, one step higher each time. The shock machine
  went from 15 to 450 volts in 15-volt steps.
- The learner was an actor (confederate). No real shocks were given.
- In this first study the learner was in another room. At 300 volts he
  pounded on the wall; after 315 volts he went silent and stopped answering.
- When the teacher hesitated, the experimenter (in a gray lab coat) used set
  prods, starting with "Please continue." and ending with "You have no other
  choice, you must go on."
- Result: 26 of 40 (65%) went all the way to 450 volts. No one stopped before
  300 volts.
- Before the study, Milgram asked 14 Yale psychology seniors to predict the
  result. Their average guess was about 1% (1.2%) going to the end.

## The antidote (follow-up)
- Milgram, S. (1965). "Liberating effects of group pressure." *Journal of
  Personality and Social Psychology*, 1(2), 127–134.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/h0021650
- When two other "teachers" (actors) refused to continue, only 4 of 40 (10%)
  went to 450 volts.

## Caution (safe framing)
- The study is famous and has been partly replicated (Burger, 2009, up to 150
  volts), but historians and psychologists have criticized parts of its method
  and how it was reported. Don't say "anyone would torture a stranger".
  Stick to: "65% went all the way" in this study.
- The 1963 learner banged on the wall and went silent. The famous
  screaming/"my heart" version is from later variations: don't mix them.
