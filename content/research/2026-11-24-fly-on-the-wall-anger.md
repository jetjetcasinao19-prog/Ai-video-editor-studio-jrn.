# Research — About to lose your temper? Fly on the wall (self-distancing)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("what to do when…" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Main study (anger)
- Mischkowski, D., Kross, E., & Bushman, B. J. (2012). "Flies on the wall are
  less aggressive: Self-distancing 'in the heat of the moment' reduces
  aggressive thoughts, angry feelings and aggressive behavior." *Journal of
  Experimental Social Psychology*, 48(5), 1187–1191.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2012.03.012
- Participants were provoked (insulted) during a task on purpose. Then they
  were told to reflect on their feelings either from their own eyes
  (self-immersed) or by watching the scene from a distance like "a fly on
  the wall" (self-distanced); a control group got no instruction.
- The self-distanced group reported less anger, had fewer aggressive thoughts,
  and behaved less aggressively in a follow-up game (a noise-blast task).

## Supporting study (talking to yourself by name)
- Kross, E., et al. (2014). "Self-talk as a regulatory mechanism: How you do
  it matters." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 106(2),
  304–324. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035173
- Using your own name or "you" instead of "I" while preparing for stressful
  tasks (e.g., a speech) reduced anxiety and improved performance ratings.
  This was about anxiety, not anger: present the name tip as an extra tip.

## Safe to say
- "Felt less angry and acted less aggressive." No numbers.
- Use a generic example name in the script (not the owner's or a real person's).
