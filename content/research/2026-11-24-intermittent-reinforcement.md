# Research — Intermittent reinforcement (dark psychology)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("dark psychology" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.
Angle: explain the tactic so viewers can spot it (defensive), not teach it.

## The science
- Ferster, C. B., & Skinner, B. F. (1957). *Schedules of Reinforcement*.
  Appleton-Century-Crofts. (B. F. Skinner's pigeon work at Harvard.)
- Pigeons pecked a key for food. On continuous reinforcement (food after
  every peck), they stopped fairly quickly once food stopped. On
  variable-ratio schedules (food after an unpredictable number of pecks),
  they pecked at high, steady rates and kept going much longer after food
  stopped ("partial reinforcement extinction effect").
- Slot machines pay out on a variable-ratio–like schedule; this comparison
  is standard in psychology textbooks.

## Link to relationships
- Dutton, D. G., & Painter, S. L. (1981). "Traumatic bonding: The development
  of emotional attachments in battered women and other relationships of
  intermittent abuse." *Victimology*, 6(1–4), 139–155.
- "Intermittent reinforcement" (alternating kindness and coldness/abuse) is
  described as one reason strong bonds form in abusive relationships.

## Safe framing
- The pigeon facts are well established; the relationship link is a
  theory supported by research, so say "it's how manipulators hook people",
  not "science proves your partner is manipulating you".
- Don't say "thousands of pecks" without a source; say "kept pecking".
- Add a help line in the description/pinned comment for anyone in an
  abusive relationship (e.g., the US National Domestic Violence Hotline,
  1-800-799-7233, thehotline.org).
