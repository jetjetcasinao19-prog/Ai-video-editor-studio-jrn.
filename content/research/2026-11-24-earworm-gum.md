# Research — Song stuck in your head? Chew gum

Niche: psychology / mind tricks ("what to do when…" series). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Beaman, C. P., Powell, K., & Rapley, E. (2015). "Want to block earworms
  from conscious awareness? B(u)y gum!" *Quarterly Journal of Experimental
  Psychology*, 68(6), 1049–1057. https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2015.1034142
- University of Reading (UK). Participants listened to catchy pop songs, then
  spent a few minutes trying not to think about them (or were free to) and
  reported each time the song came back into their head.
- People chewing gum reported hearing the song in their heads less often, and
  thinking about it less, than people who didn't chew. Chewing also reduced
  the times they heard it when they weren't trying to block it.
- Explanation (the authors): replaying a song involves the "inner voice"
  (silent rehearsal, like singing to yourself). Chewing occupies the same
  speech-movement system, so the song gets less room.

## Safe to say
- "Heard the song in their heads less often." Don't give a percentage and
  don't say it removes earworms completely.
- Don't play the actual songs used in the study (no rights). Use licensed
  or generic music.
