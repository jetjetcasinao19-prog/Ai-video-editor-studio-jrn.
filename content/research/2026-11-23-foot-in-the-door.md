# Research — Foot in the door

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: 60 s YouTube Short.

## The study
- Freedman, J. L., & Fraser, S. C. (1966). "Compliance without pressure: The
  foot-in-the-door technique." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*,
  4(2), 195–202. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0023552
- Researchers at Stanford University; homeowners in Palo Alto, California.
- Experiment 2 (the famous one):
  - Big request: let volunteers put a large, poorly lettered
    "Drive Carefully" sign on the front lawn (it would hide much of the house).
  - Asked cold (no earlier visit): about 17% (16.7%) agreed.
  - Asked about two weeks after agreeing to a small request on the same topic
    (a small, about 3-inch "Be a safe driver" sign for a window or car): 76% agreed.
  - The big request was made by a different person than the small one.

## Explanation (safe to say)
- Freedman & Fraser's explanation: after agreeing, people may change how they
  see themselves ("I'm the kind of person who does this"), so they act
  consistently later. Later studies found the effect is real but usually
  smaller than in this study.

## Don't say
- Don't say "it always works" or give a guaranteed rate.
