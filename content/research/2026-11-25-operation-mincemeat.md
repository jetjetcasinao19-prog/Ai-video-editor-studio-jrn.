# Research — Operation Mincemeat (1943)

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Sources
- UK National Archives: "Operation Mincemeat" records (e.g., CAB 154/67,
  ADM 223/794) and the National Archives' blog/education pages on Mincemeat.
  https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/
- Ewen Montagu, *The Man Who Never Was* (1953) (by one of the planners).
- Ben Macintyre, *Operation Mincemeat* (2010).
- Imperial War Museums article on Operation Mincemeat: https://www.iwm.org.uk/

## Facts (safe to say)
- British intelligence (Ewen Montagu and Charles Cholmondeley) planned a
  deception before the Allied invasion of Sicily (July 1943).
- They used the body of Glyndwr Michael, a Welsh man who had died after
  eating rat poison, dressed as a fictional Royal Marines officer, "Major
  William Martin".
- A briefcase chained to him held fake letters between senior officers
  suggesting the Allies would land in Greece and Sardinia instead of Sicily.
- "Pocket litter" made him believable: love letters and a photo from a
  fiancée "Pam" (the photo was of MI5 clerk Jean Leslie), theater ticket
  stubs, receipts, a bank overdraft letter.
- On 30 April 1943 the submarine HMS Seraph released the body off Huelva,
  Spain. Spanish officials let German intelligence copy the documents.
- Germany reinforced Greece and Sardinia (e.g., moving a panzer division to
  Greece). Historians agree the deception helped the Sicily landings.
- The real identity was revealed publicly in 1996; his grave in Huelva now
  names Glyndwr Michael.

## Safe framing
- "Hitler sent troops to Greece" is a fair short form of "German forces were
  reinforced in Greece". Don't say it "won the war".
- "Here's the trick" = small believable details sell a big lie (also why
  scammers add personal details).
