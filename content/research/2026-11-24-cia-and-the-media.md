# Research — The CIA and the media ("Operation Mockingbird")

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## What is documented
- Church Committee, Final Report, Book I (April 1976), "Foreign and Military
  Intelligence", section on the CIA's use of the US media: it found that
  about 50 of the CIA's covert relationships were with individual American
  journalists or employees of US media organizations.
  https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/resources/intelligence-related-commissions
- February 1976: CIA Director George H. W. Bush announced the CIA would not
  enter into any paid or contract relationship with full-time or part-time
  news correspondents accredited by US news services.
- Carl Bernstein, "The CIA and the Media," *Rolling Stone*, October 20, 1977:
  reported that more than 400 American journalists had secretly carried out
  assignments for the CIA over 25 years, citing CIA documents and officials.
  (Bernstein's reporting, not an official count: say "reported".)
- CIA "Family Jewels" memo (compiled 1973, released 2007, National Security
  Archive): "Project MOCKINGBIRD" was a 1963 operation that wiretapped two
  Washington journalists (Robert Allen and Paul Scott) to find their sources.
  https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB222/

## Myth vs. record
- The popular story that "Operation Mockingbird" was one big CIA program to
  control all US news comes mostly from later books (e.g., Deborah Davis,
  1979). The official record supports: CIA ties with dozens of US journalists
  (Church Committee), Bernstein's 400+ report, and a 1963 wiretap project
  named MOCKINGBIRD. The script sticks to those.
- Don't claim the CIA controls today's media.
