# Research — Defaults: how governments steer your choices

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The study
- Johnson, E. J., & Goldstein, D. (2003). "Do defaults save lives?"
  *Science*, 302(5649), 1338–1339. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091721
- Compared European countries' effective organ-donor consent rates (data
  from around the early 2000s).
- Opt-in countries (you must sign up): e.g., Germany 12%, Denmark about 4%,
  UK about 17%, Netherlands about 28%.
- Opt-out countries (you are a donor unless you say no): e.g., Austria
  99.98%, Belgium about 98%, France about 100%, Sweden about 86%.
- Germany and Austria are neighbors with a shared language and similar
  culture, yet 12% vs 99.98%.
- Their online experiment also found more people chose "donor" when it was
  the default.

## Safe framing
- These are early-2000s figures; say "in the early 2000s". Germany's rules
  have changed since (still opt-in).
- Consent rate is not the same as actual donations; don't claim Austria had
  8 times more transplants.
- Organ donation defaults are generally seen as helpful; the "dark" angle is
  the power of defaults in general (pre-checked boxes, auto-renewals). Don't
  call organ donation itself sinister.
- Governments using behavioral science on purpose: e.g., the UK Behavioural
  Insights Team ("Nudge Unit", 2010). Optional extra.
