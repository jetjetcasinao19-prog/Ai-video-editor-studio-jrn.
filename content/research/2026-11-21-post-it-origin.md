# Research — The origin of Post-it Notes

Topic type: Things & ideas / Businesses & brands
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-21

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1968 Spencer Silver, a 3M chemist, made a weak, reusable adhesive while trying to make a strong one, and couldn't find a use for it | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/art-fry-spencer-silver), [MNopedia](https://mnopedia.org/thing/post-it-notes), [3M Post-it timeline](https://post-it.com/3M/en_US/post-it/contact-us/about-us/) |
| Art Fry, another 3M scientist, sang in a church choir; his paper bookmarks kept falling out of his hymnal, and he thought of Silver's glue | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/art-fry-spencer-silver), [National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/blog/inventors/art-fry-post-it-notes) |
| 1977: first test markets in four cities, sold as "Press 'n Peel", flopped | [MNopedia](https://mnopedia.org/thing/post-it-notes), [Scott Berkun](https://scottberkun.com/2017/how-the-post-it-note-was-invented/), [Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/how-a-failed-glue-experiment-and-a-church-choir-singeras-frustration-led-to-the-invention-of-post-it/) |
| 1978: 3M handed out free samples in Boise, Idaho ("Boise Blitz"); people who tried them wanted to buy them | [MNopedia](https://mnopedia.org/thing/post-it-notes), [Scott Berkun](https://scottberkun.com/2017/how-the-post-it-note-was-invented/) |
| 1980: sold across the US as Post-it Notes | [3M Post-it timeline](https://post-it.com/3M/en_US/post-it/contact-us/about-us/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-it_note) |

## Points sources disagree on (kept out of the script)
- Year of Fry's hymnal idea: 1973 or 1974. Script says "years later".
- Exact year of the name change from Press 'n Peel. Script doesn't date it.
- "90% of people who tried it would buy it" appears in some sources only. Script says "people loved them" instead of a number.
