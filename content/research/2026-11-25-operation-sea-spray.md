# Research — Operation Sea-Spray (San Francisco, 1950)

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Facts (safe to say)
- September 1950: US Navy ships off San Francisco sprayed clouds of bacteria
  (Serratia marcescens and Bacillus globigii, chosen as stand-ins for germ
  weapons and believed harmless at the time) to see how a biological attack
  would spread. Name: Operation Sea-Spray.
- The Army's own analysis concluded that nearly everyone in the city (about
  800,000 people) inhaled thousands of the particles.
- Soon after, Stanford University Hospital (then in San Francisco) saw a rare
  cluster of Serratia infections (11 patients). One patient, Edward Nevin,
  died. The government denied any link; a federal court ruled against the
  Nevin family in 1981. The link has never been proven.
- The test stayed secret until news reports in 1976 and the 1977 hearings.
