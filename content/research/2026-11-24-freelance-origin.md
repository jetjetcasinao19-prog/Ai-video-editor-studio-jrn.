# Research — The origin of "freelance"

Topic type: Words / History (war — mercenaries)
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-24

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Merriam-Webster's earliest written evidence for "freelance" is Sir Walter Scott's novel Ivanhoe, where a lord describes his paid soldiers as "free lances" | [Merriam-Webster](https://merriam-webster.com/wordplay/freelance-origin-meaning), [World Wide Words](https://www.worldwidewords.org/qa-fre1.html) |
| The novel is dated 1819 by most sources (the OED dates the use to 1820) | [Merriam-Webster](https://merriam-webster.com/wordplay/freelance-origin-meaning), [Word Origins](https://www.wordorigins.org/big-list-entries/freelance) |
| The "free lances" were mercenaries — soldiers for hire, not bound to any one lord or nation | [World Wide Words](https://www.worldwidewords.org/qa-fre1.html), [Merriam-Webster](https://merriam-webster.com/wordplay/freelance-origin-meaning) |
| The word later meant an independent politician, then a person who works for themselves; spelling went from "free lance" to "free-lance" (1920s) to "freelance" | [Merriam-Webster](https://merriam-webster.com/wordplay/freelance-origin-meaning), [Word Origins](https://www.wordorigins.org/big-list-entries/freelance) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- 1819 vs. 1820 for the first use: script says "around 1820".
- One source cites an 1809 "free lance": not confirmed; script says "dictionaries trace it to a novel".
- Whether medieval mercenaries actually called themselves "free lances": the script avoids claiming this.
