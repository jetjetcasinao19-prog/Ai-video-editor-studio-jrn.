# Research — The origin of the Super Soaker

Topic type: People / Inventions
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-21

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Inventor Lonnie Johnson, an engineer who worked with the US Air Force and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (incl. NASA missions) | [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/lonnie-johnson), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonnie_Johnson_(inventor)) |
| 1982: working at home on a heat pump (cooling system) that used water instead of Freon | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-super-soaker-180972428/), [Biography](https://www.biography.com/inventors/lonnie-johnson-invent-super-soaker) |
| He tested a nozzle he'd made on his bathroom sink; a stream of water shot across the room, and he thought of a water gun | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-super-soaker-180972428/), [Core77](https://core77.com/posts/112239/The-Super-Soaker-was-Invented-by-a-NASA-Nuclear-Engineer-Working-on-His-Hobby-in-the-Bathroom) |
| It took years to find a company to make it; the toy company Larami finally did | [USPTO — Perseverance and prototypes](https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/journeys-innovation/audio-stories/perseverance-and-prototypes), [National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/blog/inventors/lonnie-johnson-super-soaker) |
| First sold in 1990 as the "Power Drencher", later renamed Super Soaker | [Biography](https://www.biography.com/inventors/lonnie-johnson-invent-super-soaker), [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/events-history/super-soaker-water-gun-turns-30-2020/) |
| Larami sold about 27 million in the first three years | [The Strong National Museum of Play](https://www.museumofplay.org/toys/super-soaker) |

## Points sources disagree on (kept out of the script)
- Where he worked in 1982 (Air Force in Omaha vs. JPL). Script says "an engineer who worked on NASA missions", true either way.
- Launch year: 1989 (Engadget) vs. 1990 (Biography, AARP "turns 30 in 2020"). Script uses 1990.
- Sales figures in dollars ($200 million in 1991) come from one source only; not used.
