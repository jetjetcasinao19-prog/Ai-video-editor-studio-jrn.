# Research — Operation Wandering Soul ("Ghost Tape Number 10")

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Sources
- Herbert A. Friedman (retired US Army psyop sergeant major and historian),
  "Ghost Tape Number 10 / Operation Wandering Soul", psywarrior.com:
  https://www.psywarrior.com/wanderingsoul.html
- US Army 6th Psychological Operations Battalion, Vietnam War records
  (summarized by Friedman); widely retold, e.g., Smithsonian / Atlas Obscura
  features on "Ghost Tape No. 10".

## Facts (safe to say)
- During the Vietnam War, US Army psychological operations units ran
  "Operation Wandering Soul". The best-known recording is "Ghost Tape
  Number 10".
- It played on a traditional Vietnamese belief: a person who dies far from
  home and isn't given proper burial rites may become a wandering soul,
  unable to rest.
- US Army engineers made recordings with eerie sound effects and Vietnamese
  voices acting as the "ghosts" of dead soldiers, urging their comrades to
  stop fighting and go home. The tapes were played at night through
  loudspeakers, from the ground and from helicopters.
- Results were mixed. Accounts say enemy troops sometimes fired at the
  loudspeakers, so the broadcasts could also give away US positions.

## Safe framing
- Say "in Vietnamese tradition", not "Vietnamese people believe" (not
  everyone does).
- Don't use real war casualty footage. Don't play the real tape unless you
  have checked its rights (it's often described as a US government work,
  but copies online may include other audio); recreating the mood with your
  own sound design is safest.
