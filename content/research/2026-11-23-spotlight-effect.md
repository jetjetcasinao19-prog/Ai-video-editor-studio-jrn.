# Research — The spotlight effect (the embarrassing T-shirt study)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: under-60 s YouTube Short.

## The study
- Gilovich, T., Medvec, V. H., & Savitsky, K. (2000). "The spotlight effect
  in social judgment: An egocentric bias in estimates of the salience of one's
  own actions and appearance." *Journal of Personality and Social
  Psychology*, 78(2), 211–222. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211
- Cornell University students put on a T-shirt with a large picture of singer
  Barry Manilow (chosen because the students found it embarrassing), then
  walked late into a room where other students were filling out questionnaires.
- Afterward, the wearers guessed how many people in the room noticed the
  shirt. They guessed about half. In fact, about a quarter could say who was
  on the shirt.
- The authors call this the "spotlight effect": we overestimate how much
  others notice our appearance and actions.

## Safe to say
- "They guessed about half noticed. The real number was about a quarter."
- Don't say "nobody notices" or "nobody remembers".

## Rights note
- Don't use a photo of Barry Manilow or his album art. Use a generic
