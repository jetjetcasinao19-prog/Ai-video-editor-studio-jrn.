# Research — The peak-end rule (the cold-water study)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: 25–40 s YouTube Short.

## The study
- Kahneman, D., Fredrickson, B. L., Schreiber, C. A., & Redelmeier, D. A.
  (1993). "When more pain is preferred to less: Adding a better end."
  *Psychological Science*, 4(6), 401–405.
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x
- Short trial: one hand in 14 °C (57 °F) water for 60 seconds (painfully cold).
- Long trial: the same 60 seconds at 14 °C, then 30 more seconds while the
  water warmed slightly (about 1 °C), which was still uncomfortable but less so.
- Order was mixed between people. Later, asked which trial to repeat, about
  69% chose the long one: more total pain, but a better ending.

## Explanation (safe to say)
- "Peak-end rule": people remember an experience mostly by its most intense
  moment (the peak) and how it ended, and pay little attention to how long
  it lasted.
- The tip ("end dates, talks and apologies strong") is general advice that
  follows from the rule, not something this study tested.
