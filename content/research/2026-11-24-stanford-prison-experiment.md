# Research — The Stanford Prison Experiment (and why it's doubted)

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## The original
- Haney, C., Banks, C., & Zimbardo, P. (1973). "Interpersonal dynamics in a
  simulated prison." *International Journal of Criminology and Penology*, 1,
  69–97. (Study run in August 1971 in the basement of Stanford's psychology
  building, led by Philip Zimbardo.)
- Funded by a grant from the US Office of Naval Research (the US Navy's
  research office).
- Male college students, paid $15 a day, were randomly assigned to be
  "guards" or "prisoners". Planned for two weeks; stopped after six days
  after graduate student Christina Maslach objected to what she saw.
- Popular lesson: ordinary people put in a powerful role turn cruel.

## The critiques
- Le Texier, T. (2019). "Debunking the Stanford Prison Experiment."
  *American Psychologist*, 74(7), 823–839. https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000401
  Based on archives: guards were given instructions and encouraged by the
  research team to be tough; much of the behavior matched what they thought
  the researchers wanted.
- Ben Blum, "The Lifespan of a Lie," *Medium/Trust Issues*, June 2018:
  recordings of the "warden" (research assistant David Jaffe) urging a guard
  to be tougher; prisoner Douglas Korpi said his famous breakdown was faked
  (he wanted to get out to study for exams).
- Zimbardo defended the study (e.g., his 2018 response on prisonexp.org).

## Safe framing
- Say "partly staged" / "the guards were coached", not "completely fake".
- "Here's the trick" = demand characteristics: people act how they think
  they're expected to act.
- Don't use photos from the study (copyrighted); use illustrations.
