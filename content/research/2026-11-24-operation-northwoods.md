# Research — Operation Northwoods (1962)

Niche: psychology ("dark psychology" series, government angle). Format: 25–40 s Short.

## Sources
- Memorandum, Joint Chiefs of Staff (signed by Chairman Gen. L. L. Lemnitzer)
  to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara, "Justification for US Military
  Intervention in Cuba," March 13, 1962. Released in 1997 by the
  Assassination Records Review Board (JFK Records Act).
- National Security Archive, George Washington University:
  https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20010430/ (posts the full document).
- James Bamford, *Body of Secrets* (2001), which brought it wide attention.

## Facts (safe to say)
- The Joint Chiefs of Staff approved and sent a set of proposals for
  "pretexts" to justify a US military invasion of Cuba.
- Proposals included: developing a "Communist Cuban terror campaign in the
  Miami area, in other Florida cities and even in Washington"; blowing up a
  US ship in Guantanamo Bay and blaming Cuba; and faking the shootdown of a
  civilian airliner (using a drone made to look like it).
- The goal stated in the memo was to win public and international support
  by making Cuba look like the aggressor.
- The civilian leadership rejected it: McNamara did not approve it, and
  President Kennedy removed Lemnitzer as Chairman later in 1962 (he became
  NATO's Supreme Allied Commander). Say "Kennedy's administration rejected
  it." None of the proposals were carried out.
- Kept secret until 1997.

## Don't say
- Don't connect it to later events (e.g., 9/11) or claim it happened.
  This video is about a plan that was rejected.
