# Research — Why it's called Black Friday

Topic type: History / Traditions / Words (timely: Black Friday is Nov 27, 2026)
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-22

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| Philadelphia police used "Black Friday" for the chaotic day after Thanksgiving; a 1961 article documents it | [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/black-friday-2), [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/archive/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history-philly-20221122.html), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/story/why-is-it-called-black-friday) |
| Shoppers flooded the city, and the Army-Navy football game in Philadelphia that weekend added to the crowds | [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/philly/hp/news_update/Black_Friday_started_in_Philly.html), [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history) |
| Officers couldn't take the day off and worked long shifts | [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history), [CBS Philadelphia](https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/the-little-known-philadelphia-origins-of-black-friday) |
| Merchants disliked the name and pushed "Big Friday" instead; it didn't catch on | [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/philly/hp/news_update/Black_Friday_started_in_Philly.html), [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/black-friday-2) |
| The "stores go from red ink to black ink" explanation shows up much later (earliest known 1981) | [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/black-friday-2), [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Start date of police use (1950s vs. early 1960s vs. 1966): script says "back in the early 1960s" (the 1961 article).
- The 1869 "Black Friday" gold crash is a different event: not used.
- "The biggest shopping day" isn't true every year: script says "one of America's biggest".
