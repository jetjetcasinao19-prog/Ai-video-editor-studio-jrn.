# Research — The origin of the marathon (Battle of Marathon, 490 BC)

Topic type: Ancient history (war) / Sports
Audience: US viewers, 16–34
Date: 2026-11-23

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| The Battle of Marathon (490 BC) took place near Athens; Athenians defeated an invading Persian army | [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/marathon-ancient-history-modern-origins), [EBSCO — Pheidippides](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/pheidippides) |
| Herodotus, the earliest source, says the runner Pheidippides ran to Sparta to ask for help before the battle, and does not mention a run from Marathon to Athens | [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/marathon-ancient-history-modern-origins), [Wikipedia — Pheidippides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheidippides), [JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/invention-of-the-marathon) |
| The story of a runner racing to Athens to announce victory and dying appears in much later writers (Plutarch, Lucian), centuries after the battle | [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/marathon-ancient-history-modern-origins), [Wikipedia — Pheidippides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheidippides) |
| Robert Browning's 1879 poem popularized the legend with "Rejoice, we conquer!" | [JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/invention-of-the-marathon), [Wikipedia — Pheidippides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheidippides) |
| French scholar Michel Bréal proposed a "marathon race" for the revived Olympics; the first one was held at the 1896 Athens Games | [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/marathon-ancient-history-modern-origins), [EBSCO — Marathon](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/sports-and-leisure/marathon), [JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/invention-of-the-marathon) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Whether Plutarch or Lucian first told the Athens-run version: script says "centuries later, writers…".
- The 26.2-mile distance (fixed later, linked to the 1908 London Olympics) and the 1896 winner: not confirmed in this research; not used.
- Distance of the Sparta run: not used.
