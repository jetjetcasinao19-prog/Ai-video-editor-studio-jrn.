# Research — Anchoring (the wheel of fortune study)

Niche: psychology / mind tricks. Format: under-60 s YouTube Short.

## The study
- Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974). "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics
  and Biases." *Science*, 185(4157), 1124–1131.
  https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124
- A number from 0 to 100 was set by spinning a wheel of fortune in front of
  the participants. Kahneman later wrote (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*, 2011)
  that the wheel was rigged to stop only at 10 or 65.
- Participants said whether the percentage of African countries in the United
  Nations was higher or lower than that number, then gave their own estimate.
- Median (typical) estimates: **25%** for the group that got 10,
  **45%** for the group that got 65.

## Explanation (safe to say)
- People start from the first number they see (the "anchor") and adjust
  away from it, but not enough. The number was obviously random and it
  still pulled the answers.

## Practical uses (general advice, not from this study)
- Negotiation: the first number on the table tends to anchor the deal.
- Prices: a "was $200, now $99" tag uses the old price as an anchor.

## Don't say
- Don't say "average" for 25 / 45: they are medians ("typical guess" is fine).
