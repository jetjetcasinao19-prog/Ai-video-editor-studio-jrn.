# Research — The first vaccine and why it's called "vaccine"

## Checked facts (used in the script)
| Fact | Sources |
|---|---|
| It was observed that milkmaids who caught cowpox (a mild disease from cows) seemed protected from smallpox; English doctor Edward Jenner tested the idea | [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Edward-Jenner), [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/the-day-doctors-began-to-conquer-smallpox), [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/jenner-develops-smallpox-vaccination) |
| On May 14, 1796, Jenner took material from cowpox sores on milkmaid Sarah Nelmes's hand and inoculated 8-year-old James Phipps | [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d42859-020-00007-6), [U.S. National Library of Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/exhibition/smallpox/sp_vaccination.html), [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/the-day-doctors-began-to-conquer-smallpox) |
| In July 1796 he exposed the boy to smallpox material; Phipps did not get the disease | [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d42859-020-00007-6), [Memorial University](https://mun.ca/biology/scarr/4270_Jenner_vaccinates_Phipps.html) |
| "Vaccine/vaccination" comes from Latin vacca, "cow" (via variolae vaccinae, "cow pustules") | [Indiana University Libraries](https://collections.libraries.indiana.edu/iulibraries/s/smallpox-vaccine-exhibit/page/jenner), [Wikipedia — Edward Jenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner) |

## Points sources disagree on / left out
- Spelling of the milkmaid's name (Nelmes / Nelms): not named in the script.
- The cow's name, Blossom: only some sources; not used.
- Earlier people (e.g., farmer Benjamin Jesty) had tried cowpox before Jenner: left out for length; script doesn't call Jenner the very first to try it, only tells his experiment.
- Testing on a child would not be allowed today: mentioned in tips/pinned comment.
