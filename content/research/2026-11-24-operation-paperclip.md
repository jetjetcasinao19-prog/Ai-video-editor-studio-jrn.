## Facts (safe to say)
- After World War II (from 1945), the US government secretly brought German
  scientists, engineers and technicians to work for the US. More than 1,600
  came under Paperclip and related programs.
- The name comes from the paperclips attached to the files of chosen recruits.
- President Truman approved the program in 1946 but excluded people who had
  been active Nazis or Nazi supporters. Officials worked around this by
  rewriting or "cleaning up" security files.
- Wernher von Braun: joined the Nazi Party (1937) and the SS (1940, later
  rank of major / Sturmbannführer). His V-2 rockets were built at the
  Mittelwerk underground factory using forced labor from the Mittelbau-Dora
  concentration camp. In the US he led development of the Saturn V rocket,
  which launched Apollo 11 to the Moon in 1969.

## The psychology (the "trick")
- Thorndike, E. L. (1920). "A constant error in psychological ratings."
  *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 4(1), 25–29.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071663 — the halo effect: one strong positive
  trait colors how people judge everything else about a person.
- Applying it to von Braun's reputation is an explanation, not a finding of
  Thorndike's study: say "it's called the halo effect".
