## Voice-over script (read aloud)
The man who named the Big Bang…
didn't even believe in it.

1927. Belgium.
Georges Lemaître, a Catholic priest and physicist, has a wild idea:
the universe is expanding.
And it all began from one tiny point.
Einstein reportedly told him:
your math is correct… but your physics is abominable.

In 1949, astronomer Fred Hoyle,
who thinks the universe has no beginning,
calls it on BBC radio… "this big bang idea."
The name sticks.

In 1965, two American scientists detect
faint leftover heat from the early universe.
Lemaître hears the news shortly before he dies.

A priest's idea… named by its biggest doubter.

Every legend has a beginning.
