## Voice-over script (read aloud)
Aspirin started… as tree bark.

Around 400 BC, the Greek doctor Hippocrates described willow bark
as a medicine for pain and fever.
In 1828, a German professor pulls the key ingredient out of willow.
He calls it salicin.
1897. At the Bayer company in Germany,
chemists including Felix Hoffmann and his boss Arthur Eichengrün
make a gentler version.
In 1899, it's sold under a new name: Aspirin.

Who really deserves credit? Historians still argue.

Every legend has a beginning.
