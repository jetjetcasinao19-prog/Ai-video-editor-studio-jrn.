## Voice-over script (read aloud)
At 65, he lost his business…
and was living on a 105-dollar Social Security check.

1930s. Corbin, Kentucky.
Harland Sanders serves fried chicken to travelers
at his roadside service station.
Then, in 1956, plans are announced for a new highway
that will skip his town.
Customers disappear. He sells the café.

Most people would retire.
Instead, he takes his recipe on the road.
He drives from restaurant to restaurant, cooking for the owners.
They pay him a few cents for every chicken they sell.

It works. Restaurant after restaurant signs up.
In 1964, he sells the company for 2 million dollars.

A man who started over at 65…
became the face of Kentucky Fried Chicken.

Every legend has a beginning.
