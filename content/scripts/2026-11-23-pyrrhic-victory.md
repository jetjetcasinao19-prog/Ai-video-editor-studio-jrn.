## Voice-over script (read aloud)
Ever heard of a "Pyrrhic victory"?
It's named after a king who kept winning… and still lost.

280 BC. King Pyrrhus of Epirus, in Greece,
sails to Italy to fight Rome, bringing war elephants.
At Heraclea, he beats the Romans.
A year later, at Asculum, he beats them again.
But each win costs him thousands of his best soldiers and officers…
men he can't replace.

When someone congratulates him, Pyrrhus reportedly says:
one more victory like this, and we're ruined.
Rome just keeps sending more armies.
A few years later, Pyrrhus gives up and leaves Italy.

His name became a word… for winning that costs too much.

Every legend has a beginning.
