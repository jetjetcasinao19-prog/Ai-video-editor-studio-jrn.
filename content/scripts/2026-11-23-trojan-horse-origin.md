## Voice-over script (read aloud)
The Trojan Horse isn't in the Iliad.
Homer's famous war poem ends before Troy falls.
In "The Odyssey," the horse gets only a quick mention.
The full story we know comes centuries later,
from the Roman poet Virgil, in "The Aeneid."

Greek soldiers hide inside a giant wooden horse.
The Trojans pull it into their city.
At night, the soldiers climb out and open the gates.
Virgil also gives us a famous warning, from a priest named Laocoön:
"I fear the Greeks, even bearing gifts."

Was the horse real? There's no proof.
Some ancient writers thought it was really a siege machine.

A poem's twist… that became history's most famous trick.

Every legend has a beginning.
