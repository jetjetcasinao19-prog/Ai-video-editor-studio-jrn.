## Voice-over script (read aloud)
The Statue of Liberty wasn't always green.
She used to look like a shiny new penny.

October 1886. New York Harbor.
Lady Liberty is unveiled, covered in about 300 thin copper sheets,
roughly as thick as two pennies.
But copper changes in the sea air.
Year by year, she turns from brown… to green.

In 1906, Congress sets aside money to paint her
and make her look new again.
People are furious.
One newspaper calls it "sacrilege."
The plan is dropped.

And it's a good thing.
That green layer, called a patina,
actually protects the copper underneath.

A "flaw"… that became her famous color.

Every legend has a beginning.
