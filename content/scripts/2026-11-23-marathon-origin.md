## Voice-over script (read aloud)
The marathon is named after a battle…
and its famous story probably never happened.

490 BC. Greece.
A huge Persian army lands at Marathon, near Athens.
Athens sends a runner, Pheidippides, to Sparta to ask for help.
The Athenians win the battle.

Centuries later, writers add a dramatic twist:
a runner races from Marathon to Athens,
announces the victory… and drops dead.
The oldest account, by Herodotus, never mentions that run.

In 1879, a poem makes the legend famous: "Rejoice, we conquer!"
Then a French scholar, Michel Bréal, suggests a race from Marathon to Athens
for the first modern Olympics in 1896.

A battle legend… that became the most famous race on Earth.

Every legend has a beginning.
