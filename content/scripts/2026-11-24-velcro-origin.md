## Voice-over script (read aloud)
Velcro was invented… because of a dog walk.

In the 1940s, Swiss engineer George de Mestral
goes on a walk in the mountains with his dog.
They come home covered in burrs… sticky seed pods.
He looks at one under a microscope.
Tiny hooks… grabbing tiny loops in fabric and fur.
So he copies nature:
one strip of hooks, one strip of loops.
He names it Velcro,
from the French words for velvet and hook.

Every legend has a beginning.

## Visuals — where to get them
- Film it yourself (free and best): rip a Velcro strap close to your phone's
  mic for the hook; if you can find burrs (or a cocklebur photo), show them on
  a sweater.
- Microscope view: search Wikimedia Commons for "hook and loop fastener
  microscope" or "burdock burr macro" (check each file's license).
- Pictures: Prompt Studio → free pictures (Pollinations.ai) or Google AI
  (Nano Banana), e.g. "1940s Swiss mountain trail, a man in a wool coat walking
  his dog, seen from behind, warm film photo, vertical 9:16". Don't make AI
  faces of George de Mestral.
- Brand note: "Velcro" is a trademark; saying the name is fine, but don't use
  the company's logo.
- Pronunciation: de Mestral = duh mes-TRAHL.
