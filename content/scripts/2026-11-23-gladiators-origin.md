## Voice-over script (read aloud)
Gladiator fights didn't start as entertainment.
They started at a funeral.

264 BC. Rome.
A powerful man, Brutus Pera, has died.
To honor him, his sons hold something new in the city's cattle market:
armed men fighting in pairs.
The Romans called it a "munus"… a duty owed to the dead.
The fighters were likely prisoners of war.

Soon, rich families compete with bigger funeral games.
One funeral has 22 pairs.
The games become a way to win the crowd.
Centuries later, emperors fill giant arenas like the Colosseum.

A funeral ritual… that became Rome's most famous show.

Every legend has a beginning.
