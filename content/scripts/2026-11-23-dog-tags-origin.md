## Voice-over script (read aloud)
Before dog tags, soldiers had to make their own.

1860s. The American Civil War.
Before big battles, many soldiers pin paper notes with their names to their coats…
so they can be identified if they don't come home.
Some buy metal ID badges from camp merchants, with their own money.

In 1862, a New Yorker offers to make ID discs for every soldier.
The War Department says no.

Finally, in 1906, the U.S. Army orders an aluminum disc,
about the size of a half-dollar,
stamped with each soldier's details.
In 1916, they add a second tag.
One stays with the soldier. One goes to the records.

A homemade paper note… became a tradition every soldier wears.

Every legend has a beginning.
