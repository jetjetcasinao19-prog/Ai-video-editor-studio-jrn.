## Voice-over script (read aloud)
The "We Can Do It!" poster wasn't famous during World War Two.
Almost nobody saw it.

1943. Artist J. Howard Miller makes it for Westinghouse,
a company with factories supplying the war.
The goal: keep workers motivated.
It hangs in a few of their factories… for just two weeks.
Then it disappears.

At the time, America's "Rosie the Riveter" was someone else:
a hit song, and a famous Norman Rockwell painting.

Then, in the 1980s, the old poster is rediscovered.
Copies spread everywhere.
People start calling her Rosie the Riveter.
She becomes a symbol of women's strength.

A poster almost nobody saw…
became one of the most famous images in American history.

Every legend has a beginning.

## Visuals — where to get them
- The poster itself: the National Archives and Smithsonian hold copies; the
  image is widely treated as public domain, but check the National Archives
  page (archives.gov) for its use notes before using it.
- Film it yourself (free hook): recreate the pose — red bandana with white
  dots, blue work shirt, flexed arm, against a yellow wall.
- Norman Rockwell's painting is still under copyright: don't show it; use a
  generic "magazine cover on a newsstand" shot instead.
- Pictures: Prompt Studio → free pictures (Pollinations.ai) or Google AI
  (Nano Banana), e.g. "1943 American factory, women workers in bandanas at
  machines, warm film photo, vertical 9:16".
