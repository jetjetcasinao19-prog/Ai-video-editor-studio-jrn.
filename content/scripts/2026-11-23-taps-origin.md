## Voice-over script (read aloud)
You may have heard "Taps" was found in a fallen soldier's pocket.
That story is a legend.
Here's the real one.

July 1862. Virginia. The Civil War.
Union General Daniel Butterfield's brigade is camped at Harrison's Landing.
Butterfield wants a new lights-out call.
So he reworks an older tune with his brigade bugler, Oliver Norton.
That night, Norton plays it for the first time.
Soon, other units copy it.

Its official name? "Extinguish Lights."
But soldiers call it Taps.
Before long, it's played at military funerals.
By 1891, it's a standard part of them.

A bedtime bugle call… that became America's song of goodbye.

Every legend has a beginning.
